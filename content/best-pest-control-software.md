For a pest control operator with one to twenty staff, GorillaDesk at $99 a month and Jobber at $139 a month are the only two products in this comparison that publish enough detail to work out what a crew will cost. The other four, Briostack, FieldRoutes, PestPac and ServSuite, all require a sales conversation before you learn a price. Figures were read from vendor pricing pages in August 2026 and are the lowest tier that includes online booking.

Pest control software reviews rarely say this plainly, so it is worth stating at the front. Two thirds of the products most often recommended to small pest control operators cannot be compared on cost at all, and a comparison that omits price is a comparison of feature lists written by marketing departments.

## What pest control needs that general field service software does not

Three things separate this trade.

Chemical application records come first. You need what was applied, where, at what rate, by whom, under which licence, and you need it retrievable years later. This is a regulatory obligation, not a convenience.

Device and station tracking comes second for commercial work. Bait stations and monitors are physical assets at a location, inspected on a schedule, and their history belongs to the station rather than the customer.

Recurring service agreements come third. Pest control sells a year of quarterly visits, not a visit. The agreement renews, it can be paused, and the billing is often monthly against quarterly service.

Jobber does none of the first two. It is a strong general field service product and it has no chemical application log and no device tracking. That is the reason to think carefully before choosing it despite its price advantage at scale.

## The two products you can actually price

GorillaDesk publishes Basic at $49, Pro at $99 and Growth at $149 a month. The customer portal, which is the online booking component, begins on Pro, so $99 is the honest entry price rather than $49.

GorillaDesk then charges per schedule rather than per user. Its documentation describes a schedule as one technician's route, and states that where technicians work in crews only the crew leader needs a schedule. Each additional schedule is $50 a month.

The table prices GorillaDesk on the crew leader basis its help centre describes, which means one schedule per crew rather than per person. A crew of three riding in one vehicle is one schedule at $99. Ten people working as four crews is four schedules, which is $99 plus three at $50, or $249 a month.

Pest control is the trade where that assumption is least safe. Pest technicians commonly drive alone, and ten solo technicians means ten schedules, which is $99 plus nine at $50, or $549 a month. Work out how many vehicles leave your yard each morning before you use either figure, because the answer moves the bill by three hundred dollars a month.

Jobber publishes Core at $49, Connect at $139, Grow at $199 for five users and Plus at $499 for fifteen, with additional users at $29. Automated client reminders begin on Connect, so $139 is the honest entry price.

At ten users Jobber Connect is $400 and Jobber Grow is $344, because Grow includes five users in its base. The higher tier is the cheaper purchase, which the pricing page does not point out.

## Why the other four decline to publish

Each has a different reason and each tells you something.

FieldRoutes prices on the number of active customers. It publishes two plan names, Growth and Corporate, and no figures, directing visitors to request pricing. Customer count based pricing means your software bill rises as your route grows, which is defensible and worth knowing before you sign.

PestPac is modular, and reporting on it consistently describes each module as carrying its own per user cost. A modular quote can be reasonable for a narrow need and expensive once basic service functions are added.

Briostack publishes a detailed page of add on and migration charges, including four cents a text message and $900 to consolidate a database, and no base subscription price anywhere on it. Knowing what the extras cost without knowing what the platform costs is an unusual way round.

ServSuite is now ServSuite by FieldRoutes following ServiceTitan's acquisition of ServicePro and FieldRoutes, and is positioned as an enterprise solution. Its own domain no longer resolves to a product site. A twenty person operator is below the size it is sold to.

## What a chemical record has to hold

This is the requirement that separates pest control from every other trade on this site, and it is worth being specific about, because a feature list saying chemical tracking can mean very little.

A defensible application record holds the product name and its registration number, the concentration and the dilution rate, the quantity applied, the target pest, the treated area within the property, the date and time, the technician and their licence number, and the weather conditions where the application was outdoors.

It has to be captured at the property rather than reconstructed in the evening, and it has to be retrievable years later, because that is when it will be asked for.

Jobber holds none of this. It is a capable field service system with strong scheduling, quoting and invoicing, and it has no chemical application log and no device tracking. An operator running Jobber keeps application records somewhere else, which in practice means paper or a spreadsheet.

That is the trade you are making when you choose Jobber for pest control on price. It is a real trade rather than a disqualification, and plenty of small operators make it deliberately in their first year. It stops being viable the moment you take a commercial contract, because commercial clients audit.

GorillaDesk includes chemical and material tracking, which is the main reason it appears at the top of this table despite costing more than Jobber at one user.

## The twelve month number

Annualised at the tier that includes online booking, a solo operator pays $1,188 a year on GorillaDesk Pro and $1,668 on Jobber Connect.

Three technicians riding as one crew pay $1,188 on GorillaDesk against $2,364 on Jobber Connect. Three technicians in three separate vehicles pay $2,388 on GorillaDesk, which is within a rounding error of Jobber.

Ten people working as four crews pay $2,988 on GorillaDesk against $4,800 on Jobber Connect, with Jobber Grow covering ten users at $4,128. Ten solo technicians pay $6,588 on GorillaDesk, which is the most expensive outcome in this comparison.

So the ranking between these two products is decided by your vehicle count rather than your headcount. Where crews ride together GorillaDesk is substantially cheaper at every size. Where every technician drives alone it becomes the most expensive option at ten. Across that whole range it is the only one of the two that holds a chemical application record, which for most operators settles the question regardless of the arithmetic.

The other four products in this comparison cannot be placed on that scale at all, because none of them publishes a price.

## Where each tool is the wrong choice

**GorillaDesk is wrong** for ten solo technicians on cost, where per schedule pricing reaches $549 a month and behaves exactly like per user pricing.

**Jobber is wrong** for any operator who needs a chemical application log or bait station history. It does not have them. No price advantage compensates for a missing regulatory record.

**Briostack is wrong** for anyone who needs to compare cost before committing time to a sales process.

**FieldRoutes is wrong** for a small operator on principle. Pricing that scales with active customer count means the software cost grows with the route, and for a company under twenty staff that is the wrong direction.

**PestPac is wrong** for a simple recurring residential route. WorkWave shows no price on its pest control page and routes you to a demo booking and a sales line, so evaluating it costs you a sales process before you learn whether it is affordable.

**ServSuite by FieldRoutes is wrong** at this size entirely. It is an enterprise product.

## Common questions

**What is the best pest control software for a small company?**
GorillaDesk at $99 a month on the Pro plan, because it includes the customer portal, tracks chemicals and devices, and publishes a price you can budget from. Jobber at $139 is stronger operationally but has no chemical or device records.

**Why will most pest control software not show a price?**
Because they route it through a sales conversation. FieldRoutes states pricing is based on active customer count and asks you to request a figure. WorkWave shows no price for PestPac and offers a demo. Briostack publishes add on charges but no base subscription. ServSuite offers a quote and a demo. This is common in the category and it is a real cost in evaluation time.

**Is mobile pest control software worth paying more for?**
The mobile app is where the chemical record is captured, at the property, at the time of application. If the technician has to write it down and enter it later, the record degrades. Treat the mobile experience as the primary interface rather than an add on.

**Can I use general field service software for pest control?**
You can run the scheduling and invoicing side. You cannot run the compliance side, because general tools have no chemical application log and no device tracking. Many small operators start on general software and move when a commercial contract requires station records.
