For most cleaning companies with one to twenty staff, the practical shortlist is Maidily at $29 a month for one cleaner, ZenMaid at $53, Launch27 at $75 and Jobber at $139. Maidily does not charge by the person at all: it charges by jobs completed, and its $29 Start plan covers fifty a month. Launch27 charges the same whatever the crew size. ZenMaid does not: its advertised $39 is the price before any staff are added, and it rises with every person on the team. Jobber bands by team size and reaches $299 a month across six to ten users, which is the figure that decides the question for most people. All three prices are for the lowest tier that includes an online booking form. Every figure was rechecked in November 2026.

That last sentence is the reason this page exists. Almost every comparison of cleaning business software quotes Jobber at $49 a month. That is the Core plan, and the Core plan does not send automated client reminders. A cleaning company without automated reminders is a cleaning company that pays someone to make confirmation calls.

## What cleaning business software has to do before the price matters

Four functions decide whether software for a house cleaning business is worth paying for. Everything else is a preference.

The first is online booking. A customer who has to wait for a callback is a customer who books the next company on the list. The second is automated reminders, because residential cleaning has a high rate of forgotten appointments and a cleaner sent to a locked house is an hour of wages against no invoice. The third is card payments on file, which is what turns a recurring clean into recurring revenue rather than recurring invoicing. The fourth is a crew view on a phone, since nobody in this trade is at a desk.

Vendors know this. That is precisely why those four functions are rarely all present on the entry plan.

## The cheapest plan is not the plan you will buy

Look at what happens when you price the same seven tools honestly.

Jobber publishes Core at $49 a month. Core includes online booking and online payments, but automated client reminders begin on Connect at $139 a month. The gap between the advertised number and the usable number is $90 a month, or $1,080 a year, before you add a single extra user.

Housecall Pro no longer publishes a plan table. Its pricing page is a wizard that asks for your industry, your team size and your goals before it shows any plan. Worked through on 4 November 2026 for a one person home cleaning business, it ended on Basic at $79 for one user with online booking, Essentials at $189 for five users and Max at $329 for eight, with $35 a month for each additional user on Max. The page does not say whether those are monthly or annual rates, so the table carries Could not confirm rather than a figure with no billing basis attached.

ZenMaid is the clearest of the six, and the one most likely to make you think this site has got it wrong. Its page leads with Starter at $19 a month. That tier has no customer booking form at all and caps you at forty appointments a month, which a working solo cleaner passes in the second week, so it fails the basis every price here is quoted on. Pro at $39 a month adds the booking form. That $39 is the price for a team of nobody, and the live pricing page adds $14 a month for each cleaner or office manager on it, so a crew of three is $81. The [full ZenMaid pricing breakdown](/zenmaid-pricing/) shows the figure at every crew size.

Maidily, read on its own pricing page on 7 November 2026, is the one tool here whose bill follows the work rather than the payroll. Every plan carries unlimited users. The free plan includes online booking but stops at ten jobs a month, which no working cleaner stays under. Start at $29 includes fifty jobs, a hosted booking form, automated reminders and online payments, and each further fifty jobs adds $3. The catch is that team scheduling, time tracking and the QuickBooks connection begin on Grow at $49 for 150 jobs, so a crew rather than a solo cleaner should price Grow.

Launch27 charges $75 a month on its Base plan, which includes a booking form, automated email reminders and unlimited users. Text reminders require Pro at $150 a month, and automatic charging of a saved card requires Plus at $299 a month.

## Best software for a small cleaning business, by crew size

**Solo.** Maidily at $29 a month while you complete fifty jobs or fewer, and $32 up to a hundred. You need a booking form, reminders and a schedule, and you do not need job costing or a dispatch board. ZenMaid at $53 is the alternative if you want software built around a recurring maid round, though its booking form carries ZenMaid branding until Pro Max, which is $10 more a head.

**Two to four cleaners.** ZenMaid still at the lower end, $81 for three, though Launch27 at $75 flat overtakes it at about three people and is the better buy if you sell fixed price packages online and want the booking form to take the payment method at the point of booking. Jobber becomes defensible here at $199 a month if you quote a lot of one off deep cleans, because its quoting is stronger than either. Maidily is priced on job count instead: a crew completing up to 150 jobs a month sits on Grow at $49, which is also the first plan with team scheduling.

**Five to ten cleaners.** This is where flat pricing wins on arithmetic alone. Launch27 is still $75 a month at ten users. Jobber is $299 a month on Connect at ten users. That is $3,900 a year in difference, and Jobber's advantage in quoting and reporting has to be worth that much to you. Maidily Scale is $99 for 250 jobs a month and $14 for each further fifty, so it suits a large crew only if the job count is known.

There is a wrinkle worth knowing. Connect is sold in bands of team size rather than by the seat, and the bands are what move the bill. Connect is $139 at one user, $199 across two to five and $299 across six to ten, so the price does not drift upward as you hire. It jumps the month you cross a boundary, and it does not move at all in between.

## Cleaning business management software compared on what it refuses to do

A comparison where every tool is excellent is a comparison written for vendors. Here is the opposite.

Connecteam gives ten users the whole platform free, permanently. It is genuinely the best value on this page and it will not run your cleaning business, because it schedules staff rather than booking customers and it does not invoice. It sits alongside your booking system, not instead of it.

Swept is built for janitorial work, where you bill a building rather than a person and you need cleaners to check in at a site. That is a real and underserved problem, and it starts at $150 a month on its Optimize plan, billed monthly for up to fifteen locations. That puts it above Jobber Connect for a solo cleaner and well below it at ten, because the price does not move with headcount. The catch is that it is banded by number of locations rather than crew size, and it has no customer online booking at all, so it is not competing for the same job as the other six.

Housecall Pro is the one tool here that cannot be placed on any of these comparisons, because its pricing page shows plan prices only at the end of a wizard and does not state the billing basis. That is a judgement about the page rather than the product, and it is the reason it carries no price in the table above.

## The twelve month number

Monthly prices disguise the size of these decisions. Annualised, at the tier that includes online booking, the same tools look like this.

A solo cleaner pays $348 a year on Maidily Start within fifty jobs a month, $636 on ZenMaid, $900 on Launch27 and $1,668 on Jobber Connect. The spread between the cheapest and the most expensive is $1,320 a year, which for a one person cleaning business is a meaningful proportion of a month's revenue. Housecall Pro cannot be placed on this line, because its page does not say what basis its figures are billed on.

A three cleaner company pays $972 on ZenMaid, $900 on Launch27 and $2,388 on Jobber Connect. Launch27 has already become the cheaper of the first two. Maidily is left off the crew lines because its bill follows job count rather than headcount, and the table marks it By jobs for that reason.

A ten cleaner company pays $2,148 on ZenMaid, $900 on Launch27 and $3,588 on Jobber Connect. The difference between the cheapest and the most expensive is $2,688 a year, which is a used pressure washer, a month of one cleaner's wages, or the entire marketing budget of a small cleaning company. Launch27 is the cheapest at this size, and it is the only one of the three whose bill did not move at all between one cleaner and ten.

None of this makes Jobber a bad product. It makes the question specific. Jobber has to produce more than $1,400 a year of additional value over ZenMaid at ten cleaners, or more than $2,600 over Launch27, through won quotes or saved admin, to be the correct purchase. For a company doing quoted deep cleans and move outs, it plausibly does. For a company doing forty recurring houses at a standard rate, it plausibly does not.

## What all the different names actually mean

Searching this category is confusing because the same products are marketed under half a dozen labels, and it is worth knowing which differences are real.

Cleaning company software, cleaning service software and software for cleaning companies are the same thing. They are generic labels for a field service system with a booking form, a schedule and an invoice. None of them tells you anything about what the product does. Searches for software for house cleaning business owners and for house cleaning software return the same short list.

Maid service software is a genuine narrowing. It means a product built around recurring residential rounds with cleaner substitutions, which is ZenMaid and Maidily on this page. Residential cleaning software means the same thing in different words. If you clean houses on a repeating schedule, that narrowing is worth having, because a generic tool will make you model a weekly round as a series of unrelated appointments.

A cleaning management system usually means the marketing copy is aimed at a company large enough to have an office. The product is much the same, but the pricing page tends to assume several seats.

The one label describing something genuinely different is janitorial software, which bills a building rather than a customer. That is Swept, and it is not competing with the other six here.

## Where each tool is the wrong choice

**ZenMaid is wrong** if you do commercial contract work. It is built around residential maid services and recurring house cleans, and it does not model per building billing.

**Maidily is wrong** for a crew that wants team scheduling on the cheapest plan, because it begins on Grow at $49, and for anyone who wants text conversations with customers without paying $10 a month for a dedicated number. It is also a smaller company than the others here, with a shorter track record.

**Launch27 is wrong** if you need to charge a saved card automatically without being on the $299 Plus plan. For a company running twenty recurring cleans a week, chasing payment manually undoes the saving.

**Jobber is wrong** at ten users unless you actually use the quoting and reporting. At that size you are paying $299 a month on Connect for features a $75 flat rate tool covers, and the honest comparison is $224 a month of quoting software.

**Housecall Pro is wrong** for anyone who needs to budget before committing time, because its pricing page shows a price only after a qualification wizard, and even then does not say whether it is billed monthly or annually.

**Connecteam is wrong** as a primary system, always. Free is not cheap if it cannot take a booking.

**Swept is wrong** for residential cleaning entirely. It solves a commercial problem.

## Common questions

**What is the cheapest cleaning business software that actually takes online bookings?**
Maidily Start at $29 a month for up to fifty jobs, read on 7 November 2026, with unlimited users. Among per seat tools it is ZenMaid Pro at $53 for a single cleaner. Connecteam is free for up to ten users but does not offer customer booking, so it does not qualify. Past about three people the cheapest flat priced option is Launch27 at $75.

**Is Jobber worth $139 a month for a cleaning company?**
It is worth it if you quote frequently and win work on the quality of the quote. If your work is recurring residential cleaning at a standard rate, you are paying for a quoting engine you barely open.

**Do I need cleaning specific software, or will general field service software do?**
General field service software handles the job. Cleaning specific tools such as ZenMaid, Maidily and Launch27 handle the recurring schedule, the cancellation, the rebooking and the cleaner substitution, which is where residential cleaning actually consumes admin time.

**Why do the prices here differ from the vendor homepage?**
Because the vendor homepage shows the entry tier. Every figure on this page is the lowest tier that includes an online booking form, which is one tier higher for Jobber and two tiers higher than the advertised Starter price for ZenMaid.

**Is maid service software different from general cleaning company software?**
Yes, in one way that matters. Maid service software models a recurring residential round with substitutions, which is how house cleaning actually runs. Generic software for cleaning companies models independent appointments, and you will feel the difference the first time you reschedule a whole week.

**How often should I recheck these numbers?**
At least twice a year. Every price on this page was rechecked on the vendor pricing pages in November 2026. Pricing in this category changes several times a year.
