The cheapest pest control invoicing software is Wave at $19 a month, and it is the wrong answer for most operators because it has no connection to the route, so every invoice is raised by hand. GorillaDesk at $99 a month and Jobber at $139 a month invoice from the completed stop, which is what removes the labour. Figures were read from vendor pricing pages in August 2026 and are the lowest tier that includes recurring invoicing and card payments.

Pest control invoicing is not a document problem. Every tool here produces a tidy invoice. It is a labour problem, and the measure of good pest control invoice software is how many invoices leave the building each month without a person opening anything.

## Count the manual steps, not the features

A pest control route of two hundred customers on quarterly agreements billed monthly generates two thousand four hundred invoices a year. Any manual step multiplies by that number.

Three steps decide the cost. Does the invoice generate itself when the technician completes the stop, or does someone raise it later. Does the card on file get charged automatically, or does the customer receive a request and pay when they feel like it. Does the system chase an overdue invoice on its own, or does someone build a list every Friday.

A tool that fails the first step costs you an administrator. A tool that fails the second costs you cash flow. A tool that fails the third costs you the receivables you eventually write off.

Wave fails the first, passes the third on its Pro plan at $19, and does not confirm on its pricing page whether recurring invoice schedules are included. FreshBooks fails the first and is the strongest of the five on the third. GorillaDesk and Jobber are the only two here that pass the first, because the invoice originates from the completed job.

## Why invoicing separately from the route is more expensive than it looks

The $19 option looks like it saves $80 a month against GorillaDesk. It does not, once you price the work.

Someone has to take the completed stops from the field system and create the invoices in the accounting system. At two hundred customers that is a recurring administrative task every month, and the labour cost of it exceeds the software saving immediately.

This is why the sensible structure for a pest control operator is to invoice from the field system and use the accounting package for the ledger, rather than trying to invoice from the accounting package. Wave and FreshBooks earn their place behind GorillaDesk or Jobber, not instead of them.

## The tier positions worth knowing

GorillaDesk publishes Basic at $49, Pro at $99 and Growth at $149. The customer portal, where a customer views and pays an invoice themselves, begins on Pro. The Basic plan will invoice, and the customer cannot self serve. GorillaDesk then charges $50 a month for each additional schedule, where a schedule is one technician's route. The table prices ten people as four crews riding together, which is $249. Ten technicians each driving alone is ten schedules and reaches $549.

Jobber publishes Core at $49 and Connect at $139. Automated client reminders begin on Connect. For invoicing specifically that is the difference between an invoice that follows itself up and one that does not.

FreshBooks includes automated recurring invoices and automated late payment reminders across its plans, and gates the number of billable clients instead. Five on Lite at $23, fifty on Plus at $43, unlimited on Premium at $70. Most pest control routes exceed fifty customers, so Premium is the realistic tier.

WorkWave shows no price for PestPac on its pest control page and routes buyers to a demo booking and a sales line, so an invoicing configuration cannot be priced without entering that process.

## Autopay adoption is the number to manage

The feature that decides how much your billing costs to run is not the invoice template. It is the proportion of your customers paying automatically from a saved card.

At fifty per cent autopay adoption, half your route still requires an invoice to be sent, followed up and reconciled. At ninety per cent, billing becomes a monthly exception report rather than a task.

Getting from one to the other is mostly a sales process rather than a software one, and it happens at two moments. The first is the point of sale, where autopay should be the default on a service agreement rather than an option offered afterwards. The second is the annual renewal, where a customer already receiving invoices can be moved across.

Software supports this in one specific way that is worth checking. The customer must be able to add or update a card themselves, through a portal, without telephoning you and reading out numbers. Taking card details over the phone is slow and it creates a handling risk you should not want.

On GorillaDesk the customer portal begins on the Pro plan at $99. On the Basic plan at $49 there is no portal, so every card change is a phone call. That is the practical reason the table prices GorillaDesk from $99.

## The twelve month number

Annualised, Wave Pro is $228 a year, FreshBooks Plus is $516 for one user and $1,704 for ten, GorillaDesk Pro is $1,188 for a single crew and $2,988 across four crews, and Jobber Connect is $1,668 and $4,800.

Now price the labour those figures replace. A route of two hundred customers billed monthly is two thousand four hundred invoices a year. If invoicing from the accounting package rather than the field system adds fifteen seconds per invoice in transcription and checking, that is ten hours a year. If it adds two minutes, which is closer to reality once queries and corrections are counted, it is eighty hours.

Eighty hours at any sensible rate for administrative time exceeds the entire difference between Wave at $228 and GorillaDesk Pro at $1,188. That is the case for invoicing from the field system, and it holds for any route above roughly thirty recurring customers.

Below thirty customers the arithmetic reverses and Wave alone is the right answer.

## Where each tool is the wrong choice

**Wave is wrong** for any route large enough to make manual invoicing painful, which is most of them past about thirty customers.

**FreshBooks is wrong** at Lite for a real pest control book, and wrong generally as a primary system because it has no route, no scheduling and no job record.

**GorillaDesk is wrong** at ten separate technician schedules on cost, reaching $549 a month, and wrong on the Basic plan if you expected customers to pay through a portal.

**Jobber is wrong** for a pest control operator who needs the chemical application record attached to the invoice, because it does not hold one. At ten users on Connect it is $400 a month, where its Grow plan covers ten users for $344.

**PestPac is wrong** for a simple recurring residential route. WorkWave publishes no price for it and routes buyers to a demo, so you cannot weigh it against GorillaDesk without entering a sales process.

## Common questions

**What is the best invoicing software for pest control?**
GorillaDesk at $99 a month on the Pro plan, because invoices originate from the completed stop and customers can pay through the portal. Jobber at $139 is the alternative if you need stronger quoting.

**Can I just use accounting software to invoice?**
You can, and it costs you an administrator. Wave at $19 has no link to the route, so every invoice on a two hundred customer book is raised by hand each month.

**How do I bill an annual agreement monthly?**
You need recurring invoicing with a card on file charged automatically. Check this specifically on a trial, because it is the feature that determines whether the billing runs itself or consumes a day a month.

**Should the technician generate the invoice in the field?**
Yes where possible. The invoice is most accurate at the moment the work is finished, and the further it moves from that moment the more disputes and omissions appear.
