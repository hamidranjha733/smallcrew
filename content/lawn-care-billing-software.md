For lawn care billing under twenty staff, LawnPro at $39 a month for three employees bills directly from the schedule, Wave at $19 a month is the cheapest real ledger, and FreshBooks at $43 a month is the cheapest that chases late payment automatically across a book of fifty clients. Figures were read from vendor pricing pages in August 2026 and are the lowest tier including recurring invoicing and card payments.

Lawn care accounting software and lawn care billing software are sold as the same thing and are not. Billing produces the invoice for a mow. Accounting reconciles the bank feed, holds the expense side including fuel and equipment, and produces the statement your accountant works from. Lawn care has an unusually heavy expense side, so the distinction costs more here than in most trades.

## Why lawn care billing is harder than it looks

Three things make it awkward.

The invoice value is small and the volume is high. A round of two hundred properties billed monthly is two hundred invoices, and any manual step in that process multiplies by two hundred.

The work is seasonal. Revenue collapses in winter in most of the country, and a subscription priced per employee across twelve months is paid in months when nobody is mowing.

The expenses are physical. Fuel, blades, string, mixed fuel, equipment repair and trailer costs are real lines. None of the field service tools on this page track them properly, which is the clearest reason a lawn care company needs an accounting package as well as a scheduling one.

## What each product actually covers

LawnPro bills from the schedule, which is the correct shape for a recurring round. Its client portal appears on every plan including the free one. Online payments are included on all plans, with ACH requiring Grow at $129 or Plus at $249. Automatic card charging is an add on below Plus, and included at Plus.

That last point matters for a round. If you have two hundred customers on a monthly plan, the difference between charging saved cards automatically and sending two hundred payment requests is the entire administrative cost of the business.

Wave is the cheapest genuine ledger here. The Starter plan is free with unlimited invoices. Pro at $19 a month adds automated late payment reminders, which can be set to deploy three, seven and fourteen days after an invoice becomes overdue. Card processing is 2.9% plus $0.60 per transaction, with the fixed fee waived on the first ten transactions a month on Pro. Wave's pricing page states no user limit at all, which is why the same $19 appears at one, three and ten employees. It does not confirm recurring invoice schedules, so treat that one point as unverified.

FreshBooks is the strongest at chasing money and the weakest on client caps. Lite at $23 a month allows five billable clients, which is not a lawn care business. Plus at $43 allows fifty. Premium at $70 is unlimited. Each additional team member is $11 a month regardless of tier.

Jobber ties the invoice to the visit, which is useful when a customer disputes a charge. It is not accounting and syncs to a package rather than replacing one.

## The seasonal question nobody prices in

Per employee pricing and seasonal work interact badly.

LawnPro Plus at $249 a month covers fifteen employees. If you run fifteen from April to October and four through the winter, you are paying the fifteen employee price in January unless you downgrade twice a year and accept whatever that does to your data.

Jobber charges $29 per user per month, so removing seasonal staff removes their cost, which is the better structure for a seasonal trade even though the headline price is higher.

Wave and FreshBooks are largely unaffected, because the accounting side does not scale with crew size in the same way. FreshBooks does charge $11 per team member, but a lawn care company rarely needs more than one or two people in the accounting tool.

## Prepay, discounts and the annual contract

Lawn care has a billing structure that most software handles awkwardly, which is the prepaid season.

Offering a discount for a customer who pays for the whole season in March is good business. It removes collection risk, it funds the equipment you need before the season starts, and it locks the customer in. It is also the arrangement most likely to break your billing.

Three things have to work. The prepayment has to be recorded against the season rather than against a visit, so visits are marked complete without generating invoices. The discount has to be applied once rather than to every visit. And if the customer cancels in July, you need to know what they are owed back, which requires the system to know how many of the paid visits were delivered.

No vendor pricing page compared here documents prepaid seasonal contracts. That is not a reason to avoid these tools. It is a reason to test the arrangement specifically on a trial if prepay is part of how you sell, because discovering the limitation in June is expensive.

The same applies to the more common structure, which is an annual agreement billed in equal monthly instalments across twelve months while the work happens across seven. That is deferred revenue, and it needs an accounting package rather than a field service tool to be recorded correctly.

## The twelve month number

Annualised, a three employee lawn care company pays $228 a year for Wave Pro, $468 for LawnPro Startup, $780 for FreshBooks Plus with three users and $2,364 for Jobber Connect.

At ten employees the same list reads $228, $2,988 on LawnPro Plus, $1,704 on FreshBooks Plus with ten users and $4,800 on Jobber Connect.

The cheapest complete pairing for a three employee company is LawnPro Startup at $468 plus Wave Pro at $228, which is $696 a year for scheduling, a client portal, invoicing and a real ledger.

At ten employees that pairing becomes $3,216, and Jobber Grow at $4,128 plus Wave at $228 is $4,356. The gap has closed to about a thousand dollars a year, and Jobber's ability to remove seats over the winter narrows it further for a seasonal operation.

## Where each tool is the wrong choice

**Wave is wrong** if you need job level costing, if you have a bookkeeper who expects a particular chart of accounts, or if you need to know how many people can use it, because the pricing page does not say.

**LawnPro is wrong** as your only financial record. It bills work. It does not hold fuel, equipment or payroll, and no accountant will treat it as a set of books. It is also wrong above seven employees on cost, where the next tier is $249.

**FreshBooks is wrong** at the Lite tier for any real lawn care round, because five billable clients is a cap you pass in the first week. Buy Plus at $43 or do not buy it.

**Jobber is wrong** as accounting software. It is a field service system with strong invoicing attached, and at $400 a month for ten users on Connect it is the most expensive invoice generator on this page.

**QuickBooks Online is wrong** for anyone who cannot confirm the current price first. Intuit's pricing page did not respond to repeated automated requests in August 2026, so no QuickBooks figure appears in the table above. Check it directly before budgeting.

## Common questions

**What is the cheapest lawn care billing software?**
LawnPro's Solo plan is free for one user and twenty five customers, and Startup is $39 a month for three employees. For accounting rather than billing, Wave has a free Starter plan and charges $19 a month for Pro.

**Do I need both a lawn care app and an accounting package?**
Once you have staff, yes. The lawn care app owns the schedule and the customer invoice. The accounting package owns the bank feed, the expenses and the reporting. Lawn care has a heavy expense side that no field service tool on this page tracks well.

**How do I bill a recurring round without doing it by hand?**
You need automatic charging of a saved card, not just online payment. On LawnPro that is an add on below the Plus tier and included at $249. On Jobber it is part of the payments feature set. This is the single feature that decides how much admin a round costs you.

**What does card processing cost on top of the subscription?**
Wave publishes 2.9% plus $0.60 per credit card transaction, with the fixed fee waived on the first ten transactions a month on the Pro plan. Processing fees are separate from subscription cost at every vendor here.

**How current are these figures?**
They were read from vendor pricing pages in August 2026. Vendor pricing changes several times a year.
