The cheapest lawn care scheduling software that a crew can actually work from is Connecteam, which is free for up to ten users, though it schedules staff rather than taking customer bookings. For customer facing scheduling, LawnPro at $39 a month covers three employees and Jobber at $139 covers one user with automated reminders included. All published figures were read from vendor pricing pages in August 2026.

Lawn care scheduling has a specific failure mode that general scheduling software handles poorly. The schedule is not a list of appointments. It is a route that repeats, gets pushed by weather, and has to be rebuilt when a crew is a person short. Software that treats each visit as an independent appointment makes you rebuild the round by hand every time it rains.

## Staff scheduling and customer scheduling are different purchases

If the problem is that your crews do not know where to go, arrive late, or argue about hours, that is staff scheduling. Connecteam does it and costs nothing for up to ten users.

If the problem is that customers call you to book, reschedule and cancel, that is customer scheduling. Connecteam does not do it at any price.

Most lawn care companies past three staff have both problems, and the cheapest honest answer is often two tools rather than one. Connecteam free plus LawnPro Startup at $39 covers both for less than any single product here that does the same job.

## The tier boundaries that decide the cost

LawnPro is the clearest example on this site of a tier structure that matters more than the headline price.

The Solo plan is free, for one user and twenty five customers. Startup at $39 a month covers three employees with unlimited customers. Grow at $129 covers seven employees. Plus at $249 covers fifteen.

Read those boundaries against a lawn care hiring pattern. A company that takes on a fourth employee in April moves from $39 to $129. A company that takes on an eighth moves from $129 to $249. The price does not rise smoothly, it steps, and each step is roughly triple.

Jobber steps differently. Every user past the first costs $29 a month on Connect, so the cost rises in even increments from $139 to $400 across ten users. It is more expensive at three and more predictable at ten.

There is a detail in Jobber's structure worth knowing. The Grow plan includes five users at $199 a month, so ten users on Grow is $344 while ten users on Connect is $400. The more expensive tier is the cheaper purchase, which the pricing page does not mention.

## What is behind the door in each product

LawnPro puts the client portal on every plan, including the free one. What it puts higher is automation. Sequence automations and review requests require the Grow plan at $129 a month, so a three person company on Startup schedules customers through a portal but sends reminders by hand.

Jobber puts online booking on Core at $49 and automated client reminders on Connect at $139. For lawn care the reminder matters most for gate access and dog containment, both of which turn a visit into a wasted trip when missed.

Service Autopilot publishes Startup at $49, Pro at $199 and Pro Plus at $499, and lists the Client Portal as call for pricing. Customer facing scheduling therefore has no published price at any tier, which is why its cells read Not published.

## The rain day test

Every lawn care scheduling tool demonstrates well on a sunny Tuesday. The test that matters is Thursday morning in April when it has rained for two days and you have forty visits stacked up behind you.

There are only three ways software handles this and they are not equally good.

The worst approach requires you to move each visit individually. Forty visits is forty operations, and you will stop using the software within a season.

The middle approach lets you move a whole day forward, pushing everything behind it. This works and it creates a compression problem, because you now have two days of work on Friday.

The best approach lets you move a day and redistribute across the remaining week, respecting which customers are on a fixed weekly interval and which have flexibility.

None of the pricing pages compared here documents which approach the product takes. This is the single most valuable question to ask on a trial, and it is worth more than any feature list because it is the operation that determines whether you keep using the software in your second season.

Ask the same question about a crew being a person short, which happens more often than rain.

## The twelve month number

Annualised at the tier that includes customer booking, a three employee lawn care company pays nothing on Connecteam for staff rostering, $468 a year on LawnPro Startup and $2,364 on Jobber Connect.

A ten employee company pays nothing on Connecteam, $2,988 on LawnPro Plus and $4,800 on Jobber Connect, with Jobber Grow at $4,128 for the same ten users.

The gap between LawnPro and Jobber narrows sharply with size. At three employees Jobber costs five times as much. At ten it costs less than one and a half times as much, and Jobber's ability to shed seats when seasonal staff leave closes the remaining distance for an operation that shrinks in winter.

That crossover, somewhere around seven or eight employees, is the practical decision point in lawn care scheduling. Below it, LawnPro is difficult to argue against on cost. Above it, the argument becomes genuinely close and the tie is broken by whether you quote new work often enough to use Jobber's quoting.

## Where each tool is the wrong choice

**Connecteam is wrong** if you want customers to book or reschedule themselves. It rosters people. It is the best free product on this page and it is not a booking system.

**LawnPro is wrong** at eight or more employees, where Plus at $249 makes it more expensive than Jobber Connect at $139 plus seats for a similar crew. It is also wrong if automated reminders matter and you cannot justify the $129 Grow plan.

**Jobber is wrong** for a fixed price mow and go round at ten staff. You are paying $400 a month on Connect for quoting and reporting that a repeating route does not use.

**Yardbook is wrong** as a researched decision. It is the most commonly recommended free option in lawn care, and its pricing page blocked automated access in August 2026, so no figure for it is verified on this site.

**Service Autopilot is wrong** for a small crew that needs customer booking, because the portal sits on top of a $199 plan at an unpublished price. It is built for office run operations with a dispatcher, which most companies under twenty staff do not have.

## Common questions

**What is the cheapest lawn care scheduling software?**
Connecteam is free for up to ten users but schedules staff only. The cheapest that schedules customers is LawnPro, free for one user and twenty five customers, then $39 a month for three employees.

**How does scheduling software handle rain days?**
Badly, in general. The useful capability is moving a whole day's route forward rather than rescheduling visits individually, and it is worth testing on a trial before you buy, because it is the operation you will perform most often.

**Do I need per employee licences for lawn care software?**
It depends on the vendor and it is the main cost driver. LawnPro bands by employee count with large steps between tiers. Jobber charges $29 for each user past the first. Connecteam is free up to ten users.

**Can I schedule crews without giving everyone a licence?**
On Connecteam yes, up to ten users at no cost. On Jobber each person needing access is a paid seat. LawnPro bands by employee count rather than selling individual seats.

**Are these prices current?**
They were read from vendor pricing pages in August 2026, and pricing in this category changes several times a year. Confirm with the vendor before committing.
