Pest control operators need two systems, not one. Wave at $19 a month is the cheapest genuine ledger and FreshBooks at $43 a month is the cheapest that chases late payment across a real service book. Neither will schedule a treatment. GorillaDesk at $99 a month and Jobber at $139 a month will bill from the route and are not accounting software. Figures were read from vendor pricing pages in August 2026 and are the lowest tier including recurring invoicing and card payments.

The specific mistake this page exists to prevent is believing that a pest control app with an invoice screen is a set of books. It is not, and pest control has an expense side that makes the gap wider than in most trades.

## The expense lines your field app cannot see

A pest control operator's costs are unusual and they all sit outside the field service tool.

Chemicals are inventory with a purchase cost, a shelf life and a per application consumption rate. Applicator licences and continuing education carry annual fees per technician. Vehicle costs are heavy because technicians drive constantly. Insurance in this trade is materially more expensive than in cleaning or lawn care. Equipment, from sprayers to inspection cameras, depreciates.

None of that lives in GorillaDesk or Jobber. Both will tell you what you invoiced. Neither will tell you what the route cost to run, and in pest control the difference between those two numbers is the business.

## Deferred revenue is the reason this matters

Pest control sells annual agreements with quarterly service, and it frequently bills monthly. That means you take money in January for work you perform in March.

That is deferred revenue. Until the visit happens, the cash is a liability rather than earnings. A pest control company that reads its bank balance as profit in January will overspend, and an operator selling annual agreements aggressively can look highly profitable while accumulating an obligation to perform hundreds of visits.

A field service tool records the invoice. An accounting package is what carries the liability correctly. This is the single strongest argument for running a real ledger in this trade rather than relying on the app that schedules the route.

## What the accounting options cost

Wave has a free Starter plan with unlimited invoices and a Pro plan at $19 a month that adds automated late payment reminders, which can be set to send three, seven and fourteen days after an invoice becomes overdue. Card processing is 2.9% plus $0.60 per transaction, with the fixed fee waived on the first ten transactions a month on Pro. Its pricing page states no user limit at all, which is why the same $19 appears at one, three and ten technicians. It does not confirm recurring invoice schedules, so treat that one point as unverified.

FreshBooks publishes Lite at $23 for five billable clients, Plus at $43 for fifty, and Premium at $70 for unlimited. Every additional team member is $11 a month. For a pest control route with more than fifty customers, which is most of them, Premium at $70 is the realistic tier and the table's $43 figure is the floor rather than the likely cost.

GorillaDesk at $99 on the Pro plan and Jobber at $139 on Connect are field service tools, priced here so the total stack cost is visible. A three technician operator running GorillaDesk plus Wave pays $218 a month for the pair.

## Chemical inventory is an accounting problem

Most pest control operators treat chemical purchases as an expense at the moment of purchase. For tax and for management accounting, that is usually wrong and always unhelpful.

Concentrate bought in March and applied across June is inventory until it is used. Treating a large purchase as a March expense makes March look terrible and June look excellent, and neither figure tells you anything about whether the route is profitable.

The number that matters is chemical cost per stop. It is calculable, it is stable enough to budget from, and it is the figure that tells you whether your price per treatment still works after a supplier increase.

Getting it requires two things that none of the field service tools on this page provide. You need the purchase recorded with a quantity, and you need consumption recorded against applications. GorillaDesk tracks material use in the field, which gives you the consumption half. The purchase half belongs in the accounting package.

For an operator with more than about two hundred customers this is worth setting up properly. Below that, an annual reconciliation of chemical spend against stop count gets you close enough to price sensibly.

## The twelve month number

Annualised, the accounting side of a pest control business is inexpensive relative to the field side.

Wave Pro is $228 a year. FreshBooks Plus is $516 for one user, $780 for three and $1,704 for ten. GorillaDesk Pro is $1,188 for a single crew and $2,988 across four crews, rising to $6,588 if ten technicians each drive alone. Jobber Connect is $1,668, $2,364 and $4,800.

A three technician operator riding as one crew on GorillaDesk plus Wave pays $1,416 a year for the complete stack, covering routing, chemical records, invoicing, a customer portal and a real ledger. Three technicians in separate vehicles pay $2,616. Either way that is the reference figure worth carrying into any sales conversation with a vendor that will not publish its pricing.

If a quote from FieldRoutes, PestPac or Briostack comes in materially above that, the question to put to the salesperson is what it does that GorillaDesk and Wave together do not. There may be a good answer, particularly around commercial device tracking and state reporting. There may not be. Having the number first is what makes the conversation useful.

## Where each tool is the wrong choice

**Wave is wrong** if you need inventory tracking for chemicals or job level costing, neither of which it has at any price. Its lack of a user limit is genuinely in your favour, and it does not compensate for a ledger that cannot tell you what a stop cost to service.

**FreshBooks is wrong** at the Lite tier for any working pest control route, since five billable clients is not a business. It is also wrong if you expected it to schedule, because it never will.

**GorillaDesk is wrong** as your accounting system. It bills service agreements from the route, which is valuable, and it holds no chart of accounts, no expense ledger and no deferred revenue treatment. At ten separate technician schedules it is also $549 a month.

**Jobber is wrong** as accounting software and wrong for pest control compliance generally, since it holds no chemical application log. It syncs to an accounting package rather than being one.

**QuickBooks Online is wrong** for anyone who cannot confirm the current price first. Intuit's pricing page did not respond to repeated automated requests in August 2026, so no figure for it appears above. Many accountants require it, so ask yours before choosing an alternative.

## Common questions

**What is the best accounting software for a pest control company?**
Wave at $19 a month if you want the cheapest real ledger, or FreshBooks from $43 a month if chasing late payment matters more than cost. Both sit behind a field service tool rather than replacing one.

**Can GorillaDesk or Jobber replace my accountant's software?**
No. Both generate invoices and neither keeps books. There is no chart of accounts, no expense ledger and no reconciliation, and neither handles deferred revenue on annual agreements.

**How should I account for annual pest control agreements billed monthly?**
As deferred revenue until each visit is performed. Money taken for a March treatment is a liability in January, not profit. This is the main reason a pest control operator needs a real ledger rather than an invoice list.

**What does card processing cost on top of the subscription?**
Wave publishes 2.9% plus $0.60 per credit card transaction, with the fixed fee waived on the first ten transactions each month on Pro. Processing fees are separate from subscription cost at every vendor on this page.
