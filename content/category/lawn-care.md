## The two numbers that decide this category

Lawn care has an unusual cost structure for software. The work is recurring and low value per visit, the crews are larger than the customer count would suggest, and the drive time between properties is the real expense.

That makes two numbers decisive, and neither of them is the headline price. The first is what an additional user costs, because lawn care companies hire seasonally and a per seat model punishes that hard. The second is the customer limit on the entry plan, because a lawn care round of two hundred properties is normal and a plan capped at twenty five is decorative.

LawnPro publishes both clearly. Its Solo plan is free for one user and twenty five customers. Startup at $39 a month covers three employees and unlimited customers. Grow at $129 covers seven. Plus at $249 covers fifteen.

Jobber publishes both clearly as well. Core is $49 and Connect is $139 for a single user, and Connect then bands by team size at $199 from two to five employees and $299 from six to ten.

Arborgold publishes $129, $299 and $499 a month on the annual contract its page defaults to. It offers monthly payment and does not display that rate, and it does not publish the cost of the office and mobile user licences either, which means the plan price is not the price at any crew size.

The other three publish neither number in a form you can use, which is why half this table cannot be compared on cost at all.

## What the free tier actually gives you

Lawn care is one of the few trades where a genuinely free plan exists, so it is worth being precise about what it covers before treating it as a starting point.

LawnPro Solo is free for one user and twenty five customers, and it includes the client portal. That is a real product rather than a trial, and for an operator mowing a handful of properties alongside another job it is enough. The binding constraint is the customer cap rather than the user count, and twenty five customers is roughly a fortnight of a single crew's round. A working solo operator on a full book passes it in the first season.

What the free plan does not include is automation. Sequence automations and review requests sit on Grow at $129 a month, four tiers and $129 away from free. So the honest reading of LawnPro's free plan is that it will hold your customer list and take a booking, and you will still be sending your own reminders.

Yardbook is the other product routinely described as free in this category. Every Yardbook URL tried in August 2026 returned an error, including the homepage, so nothing about its cost or its limits is verified here. It is in the table with three Could not confirm cells for that reason. A product whose pricing cannot be read is not a product a business can plan around, whatever its price turns out to be.

## Where the tier trap sits in lawn care

Every vendor in this category advertises a plan that looks affordable and puts something a working crew needs one tier above it. The interesting part is that the trap sits in a different place on each product.

On LawnPro the client portal appears on every plan including the free one, which is unusually honest. What sits higher is automation. Sequence automations and review requests, which is where automated reminders live, require the Grow plan at $129 a month. A three person lawn care company on the $39 Startup plan has a portal and no automated reminders.

On Jobber the pattern is the familiar one. Online booking is on Core at $49. Automated client reminders are on Connect at $139, which is why $139 rather than $49 is the figure in the table.

On Service Autopilot the trap is more severe. Its published plans are Startup at $49, Pro at $199 and Pro Plus at $499. The Client Portal, which is the component that provides online account access and booking, is listed on the pricing page as call for pricing. So the advertised $49 plan does not include customer facing booking, and the price of adding it is not published at any tier. That is why every Service Autopilot cell in the table reads Not published rather than carrying its advertised figure.

## What happens between three and ten employees

This is where lawn care companies get hurt, and LawnPro shows it most clearly.

Three employees on LawnPro Startup is $39 a month. Ten employees does not fit Startup, which covers three, or Grow, which covers seven. Ten employees requires Plus at $249 a month.

That is a six fold increase for a business that grew from three staff to ten, which in lawn care can happen inside one season. Jobber over the same growth goes from $199 to $299, about half again. LawnPro is much cheaper at three and much more expensive per step at ten.

The practical reading is that LawnPro is excellent for a small crew and Jobber becomes competitive around the seven employee mark, where LawnPro's tier boundaries start working against you. If you expect to cross seven employees within a year, the cheaper product today is not the cheaper product over the period you will actually own it.

## Seasonality changes the annual arithmetic

Lawn care software is quoted monthly and paid for twelve months, while the revenue arrives in seven or eight. That is worth converting before choosing, and it is the calculation almost no comparison page performs.

Annualised at the tier that includes online booking, a solo operator pays nothing on LawnPro Solo up to twenty five customers, $1,548 a year on Arborgold committing annually and $1,668 on Jobber Connect.

A three employee company pays $468 a year on LawnPro Startup and $2,388 on Jobber Connect, a difference of $1,920.

A ten employee company pays $2,988 a year on LawnPro Plus and $3,588 on Jobber Connect, a difference of only $600.

Now spread those against a season. A northern lawn care company earning across seven months pays for twelve. The LawnPro Plus figure of $2,988 is really $2,988 recovered from seven months of invoices, which is $427 a month of season rather than $249.

That reframing matters most for the banded pricing model. Jobber lets you remove seats when seasonal staff leave, so a company that drops from ten to four in November stops paying for six seats. LawnPro bands by employee count, so moving from Plus back to Startup for the winter is a plan change rather than an adjustment, and most operators do not bother.

For a genuinely seasonal operation, per seat pricing is the better structure despite the higher headline price. This is the one place on this site where the per seat model wins the argument.

## How the pricing models behave as you hire

The six products here use four different models, and the model decides your bill far more than the advertised figure once you pass about five staff.

Banded by employee count, which is LawnPro, is the cheapest model in this category at three staff and the harshest at the boundaries. The step from Grow to Plus is $120 a month triggered by a single hire, and nothing about the $39 entry price warns you that the eighth employee costs more than the seventh did.

Banded by team size, which is Jobber, holds flat between boundaries and steps at them. Connect is $139 at one, $199 from two to five and $299 from six to ten. It is the most expensive model at three staff, and for a seasonal trade the question is whether your summer and winter headcounts fall in the same band, because within one the bill does not move at all.

Plan price plus unpublished licences, which is Arborgold, cannot be forecast at all. The plan figure is real, but office and mobile user licences are purchased separately and their price is not on the page, so the published number is a floor rather than a cost.

Quote only, which is Aspire and in practice Service Autopilot, puts the model itself behind a sales conversation. Aspire at least tells you that user licences are unlimited, which is a genuine signal that it is sold to companies large enough for seat counts to be the wrong unit.

Work out which of these four you are signing into before comparing any headline number, because at three employees all four look similar and at fifteen they do not.

## What to check before you commit

Three questions decide this category and none of them are answered on a pricing page.

Ask how a rained off day is handled. The operation you will perform most is pushing an entire day forward, not rescheduling one visit, and tools differ enormously in how painful that is.

Ask what happens to your route when a crew is one person short. A schedule that has to be rebuilt by hand at six in the morning is a schedule you will abandon by July.

Ask how annual agreements renew. Lawn care sells a season, and a system that treats each visit as an independent job will make you resell the same customer every spring.

## Where each tool is the wrong choice

**LawnPro is wrong** if you need automated reminders on a budget, because they require the $129 Grow plan, and wrong if you plan to cross seven employees soon, because the next tier is $249.

**Jobber is wrong** for pure mow and go route work at ten staff. At $299 a month on Connect you are paying for quoting depth that a fixed price recurring round does not use. Connect bands by team size rather than charging per seat, so that $299 is the same bill at six employees as at ten.

**Arborgold is wrong** for straightforward lawn maintenance. It is built for tree care and landscape construction where materials and job costing matter, its entry price of $129 requires an annual contract, the monthly rate is not displayed, and its licence pricing is still not published.

**Yardbook is wrong** as a purchase you can research properly. It is widely recommended as the free option in lawn care and its pricing page blocked automated access in August 2026, so nothing about its cost is verified here.

**Service Autopilot is wrong** for anyone who needs customer facing booking at a known price. The portal is a call for pricing item on top of a plan that already costs $199 at the Pro tier.

**Aspire is wrong** for every business this site is written for. It gives no price, stating only that cost varies by company size and complexity, and its plans page is built around a conversation with sales rather than a number.

## How to choose, in the order that matters

Take these in order, because the first one that returns a hard answer ends the comparison.

How many employees will you have at the peak of next season, not today? This is the question that prices LawnPro, and its bands are set at three, seven and fifteen. A company planning to hire is comparing different products from a company that is not.

Does your headcount fall in winter? If it does, a model you can adjust downward is worth more than a lower headline price, and that points at per seat rather than banded by employee.

Do you need automated reminders, or only a booking form? On LawnPro that single requirement is the difference between $39 and $129 a month, which is a larger swing than the gap between most products here.

Are you willing to enter a sales process to learn a number? If not, this is a comparison between three products rather than six.
