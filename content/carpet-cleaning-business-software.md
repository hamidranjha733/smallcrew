For a carpet cleaning business running one to ten vans, Launch27 at $75 a month is the strongest fit if you sell fixed price room packages online, GorillaDesk at $99 a month is the strongest if your cost follows vans rather than staff, and Jobber at $139 a month is the strongest if you quote commercial work. All three prices are the lowest tier that includes online booking, read from the vendor pricing pages in August 2026.

Carpet cleaning sits awkwardly between two software categories. It is not recurring like maid service, so tools built around a weekly round fit badly. It is not a long project either, so construction style estimating software is heavy. What it needs is fast quoting, tight routing and a deposit taken before a van moves.

## The pricing model matters more than the feature list

Carpet cleaning is the one trade on this site where the vendor's pricing model, rather than its features, should decide the purchase.

Most software charges per user. A carpet cleaning company with four technicians and two vans pays for four seats even though it runs two routes. GorillaDesk charges per schedule instead, at $99 a month for the first and $50 for each additional one, and its own documentation describes a schedule as one technician's route, noting that crews riding together only need a schedule for the crew leader.

For a two van operation with four technicians, that is $149 a month at GorillaDesk against $226 a month at Jobber Connect for four seats. For a one van operation with two technicians it is $99 against $168. The direction reverses only if every technician drives separately.

Launch27 and ZenMaid sidestep the question entirely with flat pricing and unlimited users, which is why they appear high in the table despite being built for residential cleaning rather than carpet specifically.

## Where the tier trap sits for carpet cleaners

Jobber advertises $49 a month. Online booking and payments are on that Core plan, but automated client reminders begin on Connect at $139. Carpet cleaning has a higher rate of postponed jobs than most trades, because the customer has to move furniture before you arrive. A reminder is not optional, and it costs $90 a month more than the advertised price.

GorillaDesk advertises $49 a month for Basic. The customer portal and online booking begin on Pro at $99. The advertised plan will schedule and invoice, and it will not let a customer book.

Launch27 includes a booking form on its $75 Base plan, which is the cleanest entry on this page, but automatic charging of a saved card sits three tiers up at $299.

## What carpet cleaning software still will not do

None of the five tools here will price a job from a photograph, and none will hold a square footage rate card that adjusts for fibre type, soiling level and protector application in the way a carpet cleaner actually quotes.

That is worth saying plainly, because the category is frequently sold as though it solves estimating. It does not. What these tools do is capture the booking, hold the schedule, remind the customer, route the van and take payment. The quote is still yours to build.

If your quoting is the bottleneck, none of this software fixes it and Jobber comes closest by letting you save reusable line items with set prices.

## Deposits, no shows and the cost of an empty van

Carpet cleaning has a specific failure that recurring cleaning does not. The customer has to prepare. Furniture must be moved, rooms must be cleared, and pets must be dealt with.

That produces two problems. Jobs get postponed at short notice because the preparation did not happen, and jobs overrun because the technician does the preparation instead.

A van with two technicians standing in a hallway waiting for a sofa to be moved is expensive. Two of those a week is a meaningful proportion of a small carpet cleaning company's margin.

Software addresses this in three places. The booking confirmation should state the preparation required, so the expectation exists in writing before the day. The reminder should repeat it, which is why the reminder tier matters more here than the booking tier. And the deposit filters out bookings that were never serious.

Launch27 captures card details at the point of booking on its $75 Base plan, which is the cheapest way on this page to make a booking cost the customer something to break. Charging that card automatically requires Plus at $299.

Jobber places automated reminders on Connect at $139, which is $90 above its advertised price and is the tier at which the preparation message actually goes out on its own.

## The twelve month number

Annualised at the tier that includes online booking, a one van operation pays $468 a year on ZenMaid, $900 on Launch27, $948 on Housecall Pro, $1,188 on GorillaDesk Pro and $1,668 on Jobber Connect.

A three technician, one van operation pays $468, $900, $1,188 on GorillaDesk, and $2,364 on Jobber Connect. That GorillaDesk figure is the reason the table shows the same price at one and three people, because per schedule pricing means three technicians in one van cost exactly what one technician in one van costs.

At ten technicians working as four crews, which is the basis the table uses, GorillaDesk is $2,988 a year against $4,800 on Jobber Connect and $4,128 on Jobber Grow. At ten technicians in ten separate vans it becomes $6,588, and the model that was cheapest at three vans is suddenly the most expensive, purely because the unit of billing is the route rather than the person and your fleet grew faster than your headcount.

That reversal is the single most useful thing to understand before choosing carpet cleaning software, and it does not appear on any vendor pricing page.

## Where each tool is the wrong choice

**ZenMaid is wrong** for a dedicated carpet cleaning company. It is built for recurring maid rounds. It appears here only because carpet work bolted onto an existing cleaning round is common, and in that case the $39 flat price is hard to beat.

**Launch27 is wrong** if your jobs are quoted rather than packaged. Its strength is a booking form that sells a defined product at a defined price. A carpet job priced by room count and fibre type does not fit that form neatly.

**Housecall Pro is wrong** for any carpet cleaner planning to hire, because the pricing page does not publish seat limits or the cost of an additional user, so you cannot model the cost of a second van.

**GorillaDesk is wrong** if every technician drives their own vehicle, because per schedule pricing then behaves exactly like per user pricing and reaches $549 a month at ten schedules. It is also a pest control product first, and its chemical tracking and device barcoding are irrelevant to carpet work.

**Jobber is wrong** at ten users unless the quoting earns its keep. Ten seats on Connect is $400 a month. Note that Jobber's Grow plan includes five users at $199, so ten users cost $344 on Grow against $400 on Connect. The higher tier is cheaper, which the pricing page does not point out.

## Common questions

**What is the cheapest carpet cleaning software with online booking?**
ZenMaid Pro at $39 a month if carpet work sits inside a cleaning round, and Launch27 at $75 a month for a dedicated carpet operation. Both were checked in August 2026.

**Should I pay per user or per van?**
Per van, if your technicians ride together. GorillaDesk bills per schedule, which is per route rather than per person, and its documentation states that crews only need a schedule for the crew leader. For a four technician, two van operation that is the difference between $149 and $226 a month.

**Does any of this software calculate a carpet cleaning quote?**
No. None of the five tools compared here prices a job from room dimensions, fibre type or soiling level. They capture bookings and hold schedules. Jobber comes closest with saved line items at set prices.

**Do I need to take a deposit online?**
It is the single most useful thing a booking form does for carpet cleaning, because it filters out enquiries that were never going to convert. Launch27 takes payment details at booking on the Base plan, but automatic charging of the saved card requires Plus at $299 a month.

**How current are these prices?**
They were read from vendor pricing pages in August 2026. Pricing in this category changes several times a year, so confirm before committing.
