An operator with fewer than twenty staff should not buy pest control lead management software. They should buy an online booking form and an automated quote follow up, both of which are already inside the field service tools they are choosing between. The cheapest that publishes a price and includes a customer portal is GorillaDesk at $99 a month, and Jobber at $139 a month has the stronger quote follow up. Figures were read from vendor pricing pages in August 2026 and are the lowest tier including online booking.

Lead management software solves the problem of many leads moving through several stages handled by several people. A five person pest control company has one person handling enquiries, and that person is often driving.

## The real problem is the twenty minute window

Residential pest enquiries are urgent and comparative. Somebody has found something in their kitchen and they are contacting three companies. The company that responds first books the job at a rate that has very little to do with price.

A small operator loses those enquiries in a predictable way. The call arrives during a treatment. The voicemail is heard at six in the evening. The callback happens the following morning, by which time somebody else has been and gone.

A pipeline stage does not fix that. Two things do. The first is a booking form that lets the customer schedule without speaking to anyone. The second is an automated follow up on a quote that has not been answered.

Both are ordinary features of the tools compared here, and neither requires a lead management product.

## What the booking form is worth against a pipeline

Consider an operator receiving forty enquiries a month who converts twelve.

Adding a stage to a pipeline does not change that number, because nothing was being forgotten in a tracked stage. It was being lost between the voicemail and the callback.

A booking form removes the gap for the portion of customers willing to self serve, which in residential pest control is substantial for standard treatments and negligible for anything requiring an inspection. Automated quote follow up recovers a further portion of the quoted work that simply went quiet.

That is why the table above prices the tier that includes online booking rather than the tier that includes a sales pipeline. The booking form is the lead management feature for this size of business.

## Where the tier boundaries sit

GorillaDesk publishes Basic at $49, Pro at $99 and Growth at $149. The customer portal begins on Pro, so an enquiry cannot become a self served booking on the advertised entry plan. Each additional schedule is $50 a month, where a schedule is one technician's route.

Jobber publishes Core at $49 and Connect at $139, with automated client communication beginning on Connect. Quote follow up that happens without you is therefore a $139 feature, not a $49 one.

Housecall Pro publishes Basic at $79 with online booking included, and no seat information at all.

FieldRoutes lists lead management among its capabilities and gives no figure, stating that pricing is based on active customer count. Briostack publishes add on charges but no base subscription price. Both are built for operators with a distinct sales function, which a company under twenty staff generally does not have.

## Answering the phone is the feature

Before evaluating software, it is worth measuring the thing the software is supposed to fix.

For two weeks, record every inbound enquiry, how it arrived, how long before you responded, and whether it converted. Most small pest control operators who do this find two things. Response times on calls taken live are minutes and convert well. Response times on missed calls are hours and convert poorly.

The gap between those two numbers is your actual lead management problem, and no pipeline stage narrows it.

Three things narrow it, in order of cost. A booking form lets standard treatments schedule themselves at any hour, which removes a portion of enquiries from the phone entirely. A voicemail that promises a callback within a stated window, and a habit of honouring it between stops, converts better than an open ended one. And answering service coverage during treatment hours, which is not software at all, addresses the remainder.

Only when those are in place does a pipeline earn anything, and by then you are usually large enough to have someone whose job is to work it.

## The twelve month number

Annualised at the tier that includes online booking, Housecall Pro Basic is $948 a year for one user, GorillaDesk Pro is $1,188 for a single crew and $2,988 across four crews, and Jobber Connect is $1,668 for one user and $4,800 for ten, with Grow at $4,128.

Set that against what a recovered enquiry is worth. A residential pest agreement worth a few hundred dollars a year, retained for three years, is a customer value well into four figures.

On that arithmetic the booking form pays for the entire software subscription if it recovers a small number of enquiries a year that would otherwise have gone to whoever answered first. That is a low bar, and it is the reason this page prices the booking tier rather than a pipeline tier.

The comparison that does not work is buying a lead management product on top. FieldRoutes and Briostack both require a sales conversation before you learn a price, and both are built for operators with a sales function. Adding an unpriced sales platform to a five person business to solve a problem caused by nobody being free to answer the phone is the wrong purchase at any cost.

## Where each tool is the wrong choice

**GorillaDesk is wrong** on the Basic plan if the point is self service booking, since that requires Pro, and wrong on cost at ten separate schedules where it reaches $549 a month.

**Jobber is wrong** for pest control compliance, holding no chemical application log, and expensive at ten users on Connect at $400 where Grow covers ten for $344.

**Housecall Pro is wrong** for an operator who needs to model cost across hires, because the pricing page will not support it.

**FieldRoutes is wrong** for a small operator on its pricing model. Charging by active customer count means converting more leads raises the software bill.

**Briostack is wrong** for anyone needing to evaluate cost before a sales process.

**A dedicated lead management product is wrong** below roughly twenty staff. If one person handles every enquiry, a pipeline records what that person already knows and does nothing about the twenty minute window in which the job is won or lost.

## Common questions

**Do I need lead management software for a small pest control company?**
Almost certainly not. With one person handling enquiries there is no handoff to track. An online booking form and automated quote follow up address the actual loss, which is response time.

**What is the cheapest tool here that captures leads properly?**
Housecall Pro at $79 a month includes online booking on its Basic plan. GorillaDesk at $99 includes the customer portal on Pro. Both were checked in August 2026.

**Why do FieldRoutes and Briostack not publish prices?**
Both use quote based pricing. FieldRoutes states its pricing is based on the number of active customers. Neither publishes a figure, so neither can be compared on cost here.

**What conversion problem should I fix first?**
Response time. Add a booking form so standard treatments can be scheduled without a call, then add automated follow up on unanswered quotes. Measure for a season before considering anything more elaborate.

They were read from vendor pricing pages in August 2026. Vendor pricing changes several times a year.
