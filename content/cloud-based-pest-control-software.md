Every product compared here is cloud based, so the term does not narrow the field at all. The useful questions underneath it are whether the mobile app works without a signal, whether you can export your own data, and what the software costs, and only two of these five vendors will answer the third. GorillaDesk publishes $99 a month and Jobber publishes $139 a month, both the lowest tier that includes online booking, read from vendor pricing pages in August 2026.

Web based pest control software was a meaningful distinction when the alternative was a program installed on one computer in the office. That period has ended. Searching for it now returns the entire category, which is why this page is about what to ask instead.

## The question that actually matters in the field

A pest technician works in crawl spaces, basements, warehouses and rural properties. Signal is not reliable, and cloud software with no offline capability stops working precisely where the work happens.

So the first question is whether the mobile app allows a technician to complete a stop, record a chemical application and capture a signature with no connection, and sync when the signal returns. If it cannot, the technician writes on paper and types it in later, which destroys the accuracy of exactly the record that has to be defensible.

None of the five vendor pricing pages compared here document offline behaviour. That is not an oversight worth forgiving. It is the single most important operational property of cloud software in this trade, and it has to be tested on a trial rather than read from a feature list.

## The question about who holds your records

Pest control application records have retention obligations. When those records sit in a vendor's system rather than on a machine in your office, two things follow.

You need to know you can export them, in a usable format, without paying for the privilege, including after you stop being a customer. And you need to know what happens to a decade of application history if you switch vendors or the vendor is acquired.

That second scenario is not hypothetical in this category. ServSuite was acquired by ServiceTitan, then became part of FieldRoutes, and now trades as ServSuite by FieldRoutes as an enterprise product. The servsuite.com domain no longer resolves to a product site. Customers of that product went through two ownership changes.

Ask for the export format before you sign, not after.

## What cloud pricing does to a growing operator

There is one pricing model in this comparison that deserves specific attention.

FieldRoutes states that its pricing is based on the number of active customers. That is a cloud native model and it means your software cost rises automatically as your route grows, without you choosing to upgrade anything. For an operator under twenty staff trying to grow, that is the wrong direction of travel, and it is impossible to model because no figures are published.

GorillaDesk charges per schedule, at $99 for the first and $50 for each additional, where a schedule is one technician's route. Jobber charges $29 per user above a $139 base on Connect. Both rise with headcount rather than with customer count, which at least connects the cost to the capacity you added deliberately.

## The offline test, in detail

Since no vendor documents this and it decides whether the software works where the work happens, here is what to test during a trial.

Put the phone into airplane mode. Then attempt, in order, to open the day's route, open a specific stop, record a chemical application with product and quantity, photograph a device, capture a customer signature, and mark the stop complete.

Then restore the connection and confirm that everything synced, that nothing was duplicated, and that the timestamps reflect when the work happened rather than when the phone reconnected.

That last point matters more than it sounds. An application record timestamped at six in the evening because that is when the technician regained signal is a weaker record than one timestamped at the moment of application. If the software stamps sync time rather than event time, you have a compliance problem disguised as a convenience feature.

Run the same test in a basement rather than airplane mode if you can, because intermittent signal behaves differently from no signal, and it is usually worse. Software that handles a clean disconnection often fails on a connection that keeps dropping and returning.

## Migration is the cost nobody quotes

Moving between cloud products is where the real expense of this category sits, and it is never in the subscription price.

Customer records generally move without much trouble. Service agreements move less well, because every vendor models recurrence differently. Application history frequently does not move at all in a usable form, and device histories almost never do.

The practical consequence is that switching pest control software often means running the old system in read only mode for the length of your retention obligation, while the new one accumulates history from the switch date forward. Budget for that rather than being surprised by it.

Ask two questions of any vendor before signing. What format does an export take, and does it include application history and device history rather than only customers and invoices. And is export available after cancellation, or only while you are paying.

A vendor that answers both clearly is telling you something useful about how it expects to keep your business.

## Where each tool is the wrong choice

**GorillaDesk is wrong** at ten separate technician routes, where per schedule pricing reaches $549 a month.

**Jobber is wrong** for pest control specifically, because it has no chemical application log and no device tracking at any tier. It is a capable general cloud field service product and it does not hold the records this trade is required to keep.

**Briostack is wrong** for anyone who needs to compare cost before committing to a sales process.

**FieldRoutes is wrong** for a small operator because of its pricing model, not its product. Cost that scales with active customers punishes exactly the growth you are working for.

**PestPac is wrong** for a simple recurring route. WorkWave shows no price on its pest control page and routes you to a demo and a sales line, so the evaluation costs you a sales process before you learn whether it is affordable.

## Common questions

**What is the difference between cloud based and web based pest control software?**
Nothing meaningful. Both describe software you use through a browser or app rather than installing on an office computer, and every product compared here fits both descriptions.

**Does cloud software work without an internet connection?**
It depends entirely on the product, and none of the vendor pricing pages compared here document it. Since pest technicians routinely work in crawl spaces and rural properties, test offline job completion on a trial before you buy.

**Who owns my chemical application records?**
You do, but the vendor holds them. Confirm the export format and whether export remains available after cancellation, because application records carry retention obligations that outlast most software subscriptions.

**Is cloud software safe for compliance records?**
It is generally more resilient than a single office computer with no backup. The risks that matter are vendor lock in and acquisition rather than data loss, and both are addressed by confirming export before you commit.
