The cheapest cleaning business software with a working online booking form is Maidily at $29 a month for up to fifty jobs, then ZenMaid at $53 for one cleaner and Launch27 at $75. Maidily charges by jobs completed rather than by person. Launch27 is flat regardless of crew size. ZenMaid is not: it adds $14 a month for each person, overtakes Launch27 at about three people, and reaches $179 at ten. Jobber will take a booking on its $49 Core plan but will not send an automated reminder until the $139 Connect plan, and a booking form without a reminder produces bookings your cleaners arrive to find locked. Every figure was rechecked in November 2026.

This page exists because online booking is the single clearest example of the pattern this site was built to expose. It is the feature small cleaning companies most want, and it is the feature vendors most reliably place above the plan they advertise.

## Booking without reminders is worse than no booking

A booking form takes an appointment from someone who is not talking to you. That person has not confirmed access, has not been asked about pets, and in many cases has forgotten by the following Tuesday.

Residential cleaning already runs a meaningful rate of no access visits. Adding self service booking without adding automated reminders raises it, because self booked appointments carry less commitment than ones arranged in conversation.

So the useful threshold is not the plan that includes a booking form. It is the plan that includes a booking form and automated reminders together. That is the tier priced in the table above, and on Jobber it is $90 a month above the advertised price.

## What each vendor puts behind which door

ZenMaid places the booking form on Pro, which is $39 before anyone is added to the team and $53 for a single cleaner. The Starter plan at $19 has no form at all and caps you at forty appointments a month. Pro Max removes ZenMaid's branding from the form, which matters more than it sounds when the form is on your own website, and costs $24 a person rather than $14.

Maidily puts a booking page on every plan, including the free one, but the free plan stops at ten jobs a month. Start at $29 adds the hosted booking form for fifty jobs, with automated reminders and online payments. The branded booking form and the customer portal begin on Grow at $49 for 150 jobs. Read on 7 November 2026.

Launch27 includes a basic booking form on Base at $75, with automated email reminders. Text reminders require Pro at $150. Automatic charging of the card the customer saved at booking requires Plus at $299, which is the widest gap on this page between taking a booking and being paid for it without touching anything.

Housecall Pro will not show a price up front. Its pricing page replaced the plan table with a wizard asking for industry, team size and goals, and it shows plan prices only at the end without saying whether they are monthly or annual, so it cannot be compared on cost here.

GorillaDesk places the customer portal and online booking on Pro at $99. The Basic plan at $49 will schedule and invoice but will not let a customer book. GorillaDesk then charges $50 a month per additional schedule, where a schedule is one technician's route.

Jobber places online booking on Core at $49 and automated client reminders on Connect at $139.

## The crew size that changes the answer

At one cleaner the answers cluster. Maidily $29 within fifty jobs, ZenMaid $53, Launch27 $75, GorillaDesk $99, Jobber $139. The spread is real but small enough that features decide it.

At ten cleaners the answers separate violently, though not in the direction the entry prices suggest. Launch27 is still $75. ZenMaid has climbed to $179. Jobber is $299. Maidily cannot be placed on this line either, because it bills jobs: Scale is $99 for 250 a month and $14 for each further fifty. GorillaDesk cannot be placed on this line at all, because it bills routes: ten cleaners in crews of three is $249, and ten cleaners in ten vehicles is $549.

Nothing about the booking form changed. Only the pricing model did. For a cleaning company that expects to grow past five staff, the pricing model is the more important of the two decisions and it is the one almost never discussed.

## What a cleaning booking form has to ask

A booking form that takes a name, an address and a date will produce bookings your cleaners cannot complete. Residential cleaning has access and scope conditions that have to be settled before anyone drives anywhere.

Five questions decide whether a self served booking is workable. How many bedrooms and bathrooms, which sets the duration and therefore the price. Whether there are pets, which affects both time and whether the cleaner can enter. How the cleaner gets in, which is the single most common cause of a wasted visit. Whether this is a one off or recurring, which determines whether the card is worth saving. And whether the property is currently occupied, because a move out clean is a different job at a different price.

A form that omits the access question will generate a locked door within a fortnight. A form that omits the bedroom count will generate a price dispute on the doorstep.

This is where the difference between a basic and a premium booking form matters. Launch27 distinguishes between the two across its Base and Pro tiers at $75 and $150. ZenMaid's Pro plan includes booking forms with ZenMaid branding, and Pro Max allows your own branding for $10 more a head.

Test the form by completing it as a customer before you buy. If you can book a clean without stating how the cleaner gets in, the form will cost you money.

## The twelve month number

Annualised at the tier that includes online booking, a solo cleaner pays $348 a year on Maidily within fifty jobs a month, $636 on ZenMaid, $900 on Launch27, $1,188 on GorillaDesk Pro and $1,668 on Jobber Connect.

At three cleaners the list becomes $972 on ZenMaid, $900 on Launch27, $1,188 on GorillaDesk where the crew rides together, and $2,388 on Jobber Connect.

At ten cleaners it becomes $2,148 on ZenMaid, $900 on Launch27, $2,988 on GorillaDesk across four crews and $3,588 on Jobber Connect. If every cleaner drives separately the GorillaDesk figure rises to $6,588.

Only one tool here genuinely does not move. That is the finding. A cleaning company choosing between Launch27 and Jobber at one cleaner is deciding between $900 and $1,668 a year. The same company at ten cleaners is deciding between $900 and $3,588. ZenMaid starts cheapest at $636 and climbs to $2,148, which is still below Jobber but no longer the obvious answer, and nothing about the booking form changed at any size.

## Where each tool is the wrong choice

**ZenMaid is wrong** if you need an unbranded booking form on the Pro plan, because that requires Pro Max at $10 more a head, and wrong for commercial contract work entirely. It is also the wrong choice on price alone past about three people, where Launch27 is flat and ZenMaid is not.

**Maidily is wrong** if your booking form has to carry your own branding on the cheapest paid plan, because that begins on Grow at $49. It is also wrong for a crew whose job count you cannot predict, because the bill moves with every fifty jobs.

**Launch27 is wrong** if the point of online booking is to stop chasing payment. The card is captured at $75 and charged automatically only at $299.

**Housecall Pro is wrong** for anyone who needs to know what the software will cost after two hires. Its entry tier is the most complete on this page and its pricing page will not answer the second question you have.

**GorillaDesk is wrong** for a cleaning company whose staff ride together, because it charges per route rather than per person and reaches $549 at ten routes. It is a pest control product adapted to other trades.

**Jobber is wrong** if you buy the $49 plan expecting a complete booking experience. Booking without reminders is the configuration that produces locked doors. The usable tier is $139, and at ten users Connect is $299 on the six to ten band. The thing to plan for is the boundary rather than the headcount: the sixth hire costs nothing and the eleventh moves you to $399.

## Common questions

**What is the cheapest cleaning software with online booking?**
Maidily Start at $29 a month for up to fifty jobs, read on 7 November 2026, with unlimited users. Among per seat tools it is ZenMaid Pro at $53 for one cleaner, and past about three people Launch27 at $75 flat is cheaper than ZenMaid.

**Does Jobber include online booking on the $49 plan?**
Yes, Core includes online booking and online payments. It does not include automated client reminders, which begin on Connect at $139 a month. For cleaning work the two features only make sense together.

**Does house cleaning software handle booking differently from commercial tools?**
Yes, and it is the main reason to buy a narrower product. Maid service software prices a standard clean from bedroom and bathroom counts, so the form can quote and book in one step. Commercial tools treat the form as an enquiry box, because a contract cannot be priced without a walkthrough.

**Will an online booking form actually get used by cleaning customers?**
It is most effective for standard priced residential work where the customer can see the price without a visit. It is least effective for commercial contracts and heavily variable jobs, where the form cannot quote and simply becomes an enquiry box.

**Should the booking form take payment details?**
For recurring residential cleaning it substantially reduces cancellations. Launch27 captures the card at booking on the Base plan at $75, but automatic charging requires the Plus plan at $299.

**How often do these prices change?**
Several times a year across this category. Every price here was rechecked in November 2026, and all should be reconfirmed before you commit.
