Snow removal software for a dedicated snow contractor costs $10 a month on SnowPilot Pro, $95 on Yeti Software and $245 on Service Fusion, all read from vendor pricing pages on 11 October 2026. Aurora Suite publishes nothing and routes buyers to a demo. The number that matters is not in that list: three of these four charge the same whatever your headcount, so the question that decides your bill is how many sites you hold, not how many people drive.

That inverts the usual advice. Most software comparisons, including most of the pages on this site, turn on seats. A snow contractor who hires four drivers in November and lets them go in April is the buyer least well served by per seat pricing, and the vendors that serve this trade have largely stopped using it.

## Why a snow only operator is a different buyer

If you mow in summer and plow in winter, you are solving a different problem and should read [lawn care and snow removal software](/lawn-care-snow-removal-software/) instead. That page is about carrying one system across two seasons and what the twelve month number comes to.

This page assumes snow is the whole business. You have a book of properties, a season that lasts five months, and a billing model that is either per push, per inch, seasonal flat rate or time and materials. Nothing about your year looks like a scheduled lawn round.

## The bill is driven by sites, not staff

This is the finding, and it is worth stating plainly because it is the opposite of every other comparison on this site. Of the four tools here, only one publishes a figure that changes when you hire. SnowPilot Pro is $10 a month with unlimited crew accounts. Yeti Software includes unlimited users at every band. Service Fusion states unlimited users on all three tiers. Headcount is simply not the meter.

Yeti is the one that publishes a full ladder, and it charges by the number of sites under management. That ladder is the most useful thing on this page, and it does not fit in a one line note, so it is set out in full below.

Two things about that ladder are worth knowing before you budget. The figure Yeti leads with is $66.50, which is the annual rate with the slider resting on one site. Paying month to month at the same band is $95, because annual billing is a flat 30 per cent discount at every step. And the bands are coarse at the bottom. Your twenty fifth site is free and your twenty sixth costs $150 a month, because it moves you from the 1 to 25 band to the 26 to 50 band. If you are near that line, the cheapest thing you can do is know where it is.

## Per push and seasonal contract billing

Snow billing is the part general field service software gets wrong, and it is worth being specific about what to look for.

Per push charges for each visit, which needs a system that records a visit as a billable event and bundles the month into one invoice. Seasonal charges a flat rate across the winter regardless of how often it snows, which needs recurring billing that keeps running in a month when nothing happened. Per inch bands by accumulation. Time and materials charges hours plus salt.

SnowPilot Pro is the only one of the four whose pricing page describes both of the first two directly, offering one off pushes scheduled storm by storm or a seasonal contract that invoices the same amount every month of the season on its own. Yeti describes planning, running, proving and billing every storm from one platform, and includes subcontractor management, which matters if you push work to other trucks.

Service Fusion is the awkward one. It lists Progressive Billing and Recurring Invoicing, which is the capability a seasonal contract needs, but on its pricing page that item sits under Add-Ons on Starter, Plus and Pro alike, and no price is published for it at any tier. So the $245 entry figure is the cost of the platform without the billing model this trade runs on, and the real number is unpublished.

## Proof of service, which is really insurance

Every one of these tools sells time stamped photographs of a cleared property. That is not a convenience feature in snow work. A slip and fall claim arrives months after the storm, and the question is whether you can show the lot was cleared and when. SnowPilot Pro ties photos to a driver check in and records a skip with a reason. Yeti allows up to 200 photos per site visit on its single plan and stores service history records without a limit.

If you hold commercial contracts, ask what the export looks like before you buy. A photograph you cannot produce in a form a lawyer accepts is not evidence.

## What a season actually costs

A five month season is the honest unit, because most of this software is month to month and a snow contractor does not need it in July.

Fifty sites on Yeti, paying monthly, is $245 a month and $1,225 for a five month season. The same fifty sites on an annual plan is $171.50 a month but you buy twelve months, so it is $2,058, and the annual rate is only the cheaper option if you keep using the system through summer. That is a real decision rather than a trick, and it is the reverse of the usual advice to take the annual discount.

SnowPilot Pro at $10 a month is $50 across the same season, which makes the annual question irrelevant. Service Fusion at $245 monthly is $1,225 for five months before the add-ons the seasonal billing model needs.

## Dispatch at three in the morning

Dispatch is the other half of this trade and it is covered in detail on [lawn care and snow removal software](/lawn-care-snow-removal-software/), which compares the tools that handle a route triggered by weather rather than a calendar. The short version for a snow only operator is that all four here put tonight's stops in run order on a driver's phone, and none of their pricing pages is the place to judge how well.

## Where each tool is the wrong choice

**SnowPilot Pro is wrong** for a contractor who needs a vendor with a long track record. It is the cheapest option here by a wide margin and the feature list is specific rather than vague, but it is a small product and the pricing page carries no company address or phone number, which the others do. Run the trial before you move your book across.

**Yeti Software is wrong** for an operator with a handful of driveways. At $95 a month billed monthly it costs more per site at the bottom of its first band than almost anything else here, because that band stretches from one site to twenty five and you pay the same at either end.

**Service Fusion is wrong** for a snow only business at this size. It is built to run several trades year round, the entry price is $245 a month, and the recurring invoicing a seasonal contract depends on is an unpriced add on rather than part of the plan.

**Aurora Suite is wrong** for anyone comparing on price, because there is nothing to compare. Its site has no pricing page, and the only route to a number is a demo.

## Common questions

**What software do snow plowing companies use?**
The tools built specifically for the trade are Yeti Software, SnowPilot Pro and Aurora Suite, and general field service platforms such as Service Fusion are also sold into it. The split that matters is that the snow specific tools charge by sites or a flat rate, while general platforms charge by tier.

**How much does snow removal software cost?**
From $10 a month on SnowPilot Pro to $245 on Service Fusion, read on 11 October 2026. Yeti Software is $95 a month for up to twenty five sites paying monthly, rising to $945 at five hundred sites.

**Does snow removal software handle per push and seasonal billing?**
SnowPilot Pro states both directly on its pricing page. Yeti describes billing every storm from one platform. Service Fusion lists recurring invoicing as an add on at every tier without publishing its price, so the configuration a seasonal contract needs has no published cost.

**Is it cheaper to pay annually?**
Only if you use the software all year. Yeti discounts 30 per cent for annual billing, but a five month season paid monthly is $1,225 at fifty sites against $2,058 for twelve months at the annual rate.

**How current are these prices?**
All four were read from vendor pricing pages on 11 October 2026, with the Yeti site slider and the Yeti and Service Fusion billing toggles driven rather than read at their defaults. Pricing in this category changes several times a year.
