No product compared here publishes evidence that it handles snow removal billing properly, and that is the finding a two season business needs before it spends anything. LawnPro at $39 a month for three employees and Jobber at $139 a month are the cheapest credible ways to run both seasons, but neither vendor's pricing page documents per push billing or seasonal contract caps. Figures were read from vendor pricing pages in August 2026 and are the lowest tier including online booking.

A lawn care company that plows in winter is running two businesses with different economics inside one company. Software sold for lawn care handles the summer half well and the winter half by accident.

## Why snow breaks lawn care software

Lawn care is scheduled. You know on Monday what Thursday looks like. Snow is triggered. The route exists only because it snowed, and it starts at three in the morning with whoever answers the phone.

Lawn care billing is per visit or per month at a known rate. Snow billing is one of several models that lawn software does not have a field for. Per push charges for each visit. Per inch charges by accumulation band. Seasonal charges a flat rate for the winter regardless of events, often with a cap on the number of pushes after which extra visits are chargeable. Time and materials charges for hours plus salt.

A seasonal contract with a push cap is the one that causes real damage. You need to know, in February, how many pushes each customer has consumed against their cap, because past the cap the visits become billable. No pricing page compared here mentions that capability, and a system that cannot count pushes against a cap will quietly lose you money all winter.

## What the two season structure costs

The cost problem is seasonal headcount meeting per seat pricing.

Jobber charges $29 per user per month on top of a $139 Connect base. A company with four summer staff and eight winter staff pays for eight seats in January and four in July, which is the correct behaviour, provided you remember to remove the seats. That is the strongest argument for per seat pricing in this trade, and it is the opposite of the argument that applies elsewhere on this site.

LawnPro bands by employee count. Startup at $39 covers three employees, Grow at $129 covers seven, Plus at $249 covers fifteen. A company that runs three in summer and eight in winter is a $39 business for seven months and a $249 business for five, and moving between bands twice a year is a decision rather than an adjustment.

Arborgold publishes plan prices of $129, $299 and $499 and does not publish licence costs, so a seasonal headcount cannot be modelled from its pricing page at all.

## What to test before you buy

Because none of the vendor pricing pages document snow billing, this is a category where a trial has to answer the questions the marketing does not.

Ask whether a customer record can hold a seasonal contract with a push cap and count consumed pushes against it. Ask whether an entire route can be created and dispatched at short notice without building each visit by hand. Ask whether salt and material use can be recorded per property, because material is the cost that separates a profitable plow contract from a loss. Ask whether a visit can be invoiced immediately at a per push rate while another customer on the same route is on a seasonal contract and is not invoiced at all.

If a vendor cannot demonstrate the last one, it is a lawn care product with snow in the marketing copy.

## Dispatching a route at three in the morning

The operational problem specific to snow is that the work begins while everyone is asleep and the decision to begin is yours.

At two in the morning you decide the accumulation justifies a push. By three you need every driver to know they are working, which properties they have, in what order, and what the salt instruction is for each one. By seven the commercial properties must be clear because staff are arriving.

That sequence has three software requirements and they are different from anything lawn care needs.

The first is a route that can be activated rather than built. The winter route should already exist, dormant, and dispatching it should be one action rather than the creation of forty visits.

The second is notification that reaches a sleeping driver. An in app notification is not sufficient at three in the morning.

The third is a completion record with a timestamp and, ideally, a photograph. Snow removal generates liability claims. A commercial client whose customer falls in the car park will ask when you cleared it, and a timestamped record with a photograph is the difference between a defended claim and a paid one.

None of the vendor pricing pages compared here documents any of the three. Test all of them before winter, not during it.

## The twelve month number

Annualised, a three employee two season operation pays $468 a year on LawnPro Startup and $2,364 on Jobber Connect. At ten employees it pays $2,988 on LawnPro Plus and $4,800 on Jobber Connect, with Grow at $4,128. Arborgold Starter is $1,548 a year for one user with licence costs unpublished.

The seasonal swing changes how to read those numbers. A company running four staff in summer and nine in winter pays Jobber for four seats in July and nine in January, which annualises to roughly $3,600 rather than the $4,452 that nine seats all year would cost.

The same company on LawnPro sits in the Grow band at $129 for up to seven employees in summer and the Plus band at $249 for up to fifteen in winter, which is roughly $2,200 a year if you actually change bands twice, and $2,988 if you do not.

For a two season business, the software that lets you shed capacity in the off season is worth more than its headline price suggests. That is the opposite of the conclusion most pages on this site reach, and it is the correct one here.

## Where each tool is the wrong choice

**LawnPro is wrong** if snow is more than an occasional add on. Nothing on its pricing page indicates per push or capped seasonal billing, and its employee bands suit a stable headcount rather than a seasonal swing.

**Jobber is wrong** for pure route work at ten staff, at $400 a month on Connect. It is the best structured of these for seasonal headcount because seats can be added and removed, and its Grow plan at $199 for five users makes ten users $344 rather than $400.

**Arborgold is wrong** for straightforward plowing. It is built for tree care and landscape construction with material tracking, which is relevant to salt, but its licence pricing is unpublished so you cannot budget a winter crew.

**Yardbook is wrong** as a researched purchase, because its pricing page blocked automated access in August 2026.

**Service Autopilot is wrong** for a company without an office. Overnight dispatch is a genuine strength, and the customer portal is a call for pricing item on top of a $199 plan, so the configuration you need has no published cost.

## Common questions

**Is there software that handles both lawn care and snow removal?**
All five products here will schedule and invoice both. None of their pricing pages documents per push billing or seasonal push caps, which is the part of snow work that differs from lawn care. Verify it on a trial.

**How should I bill snow removal?**
The common models are per push, per inch, seasonal flat rate with a push cap, and time plus materials. The one that needs software support is the seasonal cap, because you must count consumed pushes against the cap to bill the overage.

**Does per seat pricing work for a seasonal business?**
Better than banded pricing, provided you actually remove seats at the end of the season. Jobber charges $29 per user per month, so a winter only hire costs you five months rather than twelve.

**What is the cheapest way to run both seasons?**
LawnPro at $39 a month for up to three employees, checked in August 2026. Above seven employees the next band is $249 and Jobber becomes competitive.

**How current are these prices?**
They were read from vendor pricing pages in August 2026. Vendor pricing changes several times a year.
