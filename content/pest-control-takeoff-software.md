None of the five products compared here performs a measured takeoff from a plan. Takeoff is a construction estimating function, and pest control operators who need it are usually bidding termite pretreatment on new construction, where the measurement comes from the builder's drawings rather than from pest control software. GorillaDesk at $99 a month and Jobber at $139 a month will hold the resulting quote and bill the job. Figures were read from vendor pricing pages in August 2026 and are the lowest tier including online booking.

This is a small search term with a genuine problem behind it, and it is served almost entirely by pages that pretend the category exists. It does not, in the form the phrase implies.

## What people mean when they search for this

Three different problems arrive at this phrase.

The first is termite pretreatment bidding on new construction. You are given a set of plans and you need the slab area or the linear footage of the foundation to calculate chemical volume and price the job. This is a genuine takeoff and it is a construction estimating task.

The second is commercial bid diagramming. You are bidding a warehouse or a food facility and you need to show where bait stations and monitors will be placed, both to price the job and to define the service afterwards. This is a floor plan and device placement problem rather than a measurement one.

The third is residential quoting by house size, where an operator wants perimeter linear footage without visiting. This is usually solved with public property records or a satellite measurement tool rather than pest control software.

They are three different needs and no single product on this page addresses all three.

## What the pest control products actually offer

Briostack publishes a product page for bids and diagramming software, which is the closest thing in this comparison to the second problem. Its published charges cover add ons and migration work rather than the platform itself, so there is no base price to compare on and a sales conversation is the only route to one.

WorkWave shows no price for PestPac and routes buyers to a demo and a sales line, so a bidding or diagramming configuration cannot be costed from published material.

FieldRoutes publishes plan names and no prices, stating that pricing is based on active customer count.

GorillaDesk and Jobber both publish prices and neither documents measured takeoff on its pricing page. What they do well is hold the quote once you have the number, convert it to a job, schedule it and invoice it.

That division is the practical answer. Measure in a tool built for measuring. Quote, schedule and bill in the field service system you already pay for.

## The honest recommendation

For termite pretreat work on new construction, take the measurement from the builder's plans. The general contractor has them, they are dimensioned, and the slab area is the number you need. A dedicated construction takeoff product is worth buying only if pretreat work is a substantial and regular part of your revenue rather than an occasional job.

For commercial bid diagramming, a floor plan with device positions marked is what wins the contract and defines the service afterwards. Briostack publishes a product for this and no price. Many operators do it in a drawing tool and attach the result to the quote.

For residential perimeter estimates, use property records or a satellite measurement tool, then hold the resulting price in your field service system.

In all three cases the pest control software is downstream of the measurement, and buying a more expensive pest control product will not move the measurement upstream.

## Working from the builder's plans

For termite pretreatment, the measurement you need already exists and somebody else has paid to have it drawn.

A residential construction set includes a foundation plan with dimensions. From it you take the slab area in square feet for a horizontal treatment, and the linear footage of the foundation perimeter plus any interior footings for a trench and treat application.

Ask the general contractor for the foundation plan rather than the full set. It is one sheet, it is dimensioned, and it is the sheet that answers the question. Requesting the whole set slows everyone down and gets you drawings you will not read.

Two details change the number and are worth confirming before quoting. Whether the slab is monolithic or has separate footings, because that changes the linear footage substantially. And whether there is a basement or crawl space, because that changes the treatment entirely.

From the area and the linear footage, chemical volume follows from your product label rate. The label is the authority on dilution and application rate, and it is the document your quote should be built from rather than any software default.

That is the whole takeoff. It is a sheet of paper, a scale and a calculator, and for the volume of pretreat work a company under twenty staff does, it does not justify a software purchase.

## When dedicated takeoff software is worth buying

There is a threshold, and it is worth naming so this page is not simply discouraging.

Dedicated construction takeoff software becomes worth buying when pretreat work is regular rather than occasional, when you are bidding multi unit developments where the same plan repeats across dozens of buildings, and when you are competing against companies that turn bids around within a day.

At that point the value is speed rather than accuracy. Digital measurement from a PDF plan is considerably faster than a scale rule, and on a development with forty identical units the saving compounds.

Below that threshold, which covers most operators this site is written for, the measurement takes twenty minutes and happens a handful of times a year. Software that costs a monthly subscription to save twenty minutes several times a year does not pay for itself, and the operators selling it know that.

If pretreat becomes a genuine revenue line, revisit the question. Until it does, take the number off the builder's plan and put the quote into the field service system you already pay for.

## Where each tool is the wrong choice

**GorillaDesk is wrong** if you expected takeoff. Nothing on its pricing page describes measurement from plans. It is the cheapest complete pest control system here with a published price, and that is a different claim.

**Jobber is wrong** for pest control generally, holding no chemical application log, and it performs no measurement of any kind.

**Briostack is wrong** for anyone who needs to know the cost before investing time in a sales process, which for a five person operator evaluating a niche capability is most people.

**PestPac is wrong** for a small operator, since a modular quote for a bidding capability is likely to price against a larger business.

**FieldRoutes is wrong** at this size on its pricing model alone, charging by active customer count with no published figures.

**Buying dedicated takeoff software is wrong** unless new construction pretreat is a regular revenue line rather than an occasional job.
