For a lawn care business with one to twenty staff, LawnPro is the cheapest credible option at $39 a month for three employees, and Jobber is the strongest at $139 a month for one user rising to $400 at ten. Three of the six most recommended products in this category, Service Autopilot, Aspire and Yardbook, could not be priced for a crew of three from their own published material in August 2026.

That is the finding that matters. Lawn care software management is a category where roughly half the market declines to tell you what it costs until you speak to someone, and the review sites that rank these products quote starting prices that do not include the features a lawn care business needs.

## The two numbers that decide this category

Lawn care has an unusual cost structure for software. The work is recurring and low value per visit, the crews are larger than the customer count would suggest, and the drive time between properties is the real expense.

That makes two numbers decisive. The first is what an additional user costs, because lawn care companies hire seasonally and a per seat model punishes that hard. The second is the customer limit on the entry plan, because a lawn care round of two hundred properties is normal and a plan capped at twenty five is decorative.

LawnPro publishes both clearly. Its Solo plan is free for one user and twenty five customers. Startup at $39 a month covers three employees and unlimited customers. Grow at $129 covers seven. Plus at $249 covers fifteen.

Jobber publishes both clearly as well. Core is $49, Connect is $139, and every user past the first is $29 a month.

Arborgold publishes plan prices of $129, $299 and $499 a month and does not publish what a user licence costs, which means the plan price is not the price.

## Where the tier trap sits in lawn care

On LawnPro the client portal appears on every plan including the free one, which is unusually honest. What sits higher is automation. Sequence automations and review requests, which is where automated reminders live, require the Grow plan at $129 a month. A three person lawn care company on the $39 Startup plan has a portal and no automated reminders.

On Jobber the pattern is the familiar one. Online booking is on Core at $49. Automated client reminders are on Connect at $139.

On Service Autopilot the trap is more severe. Its published plans are Startup at $49, Pro at $199 and Pro Plus at $499. The Client Portal, which is the component that provides online account access and booking, is listed on the pricing page as call for pricing. So the advertised $49 plan does not include customer facing booking, and the price of adding it is not published at any tier. That is why every Service Autopilot cell in the table reads Not published.

## What happens between three and ten employees

This is where lawn care companies get hurt, and LawnPro shows it most clearly.

Three employees on LawnPro Startup is $39 a month. Ten employees does not fit Startup, which covers three, or Grow, which covers seven. Ten employees requires Plus at $249 a month.

That is a six fold increase for a business that grew from three staff to ten, which in lawn care can happen inside one season. Jobber over the same growth goes from $197 to $400, roughly double. LawnPro is much cheaper at three and much more expensive per step at ten.

The practical reading is that LawnPro is excellent for a small crew and Jobber becomes competitive around the seven employee mark, where LawnPro's tier boundaries start working against you.

## Seasonality changes the annual arithmetic

Lawn care software is quoted monthly and paid for twelve months, while the revenue arrives in seven or eight. That is worth converting before choosing.

Annualised at the tier that includes online booking, a solo operator pays nothing on LawnPro Solo up to twenty five customers, $1,548 a year on Arborgold Starter and $1,668 on Jobber Connect.

A three employee company pays $468 a year on LawnPro Startup and $2,364 on Jobber Connect.

A ten employee company pays $2,988 a year on LawnPro Plus and $4,800 on Jobber Connect, with Jobber Grow covering ten users at $4,128.

Now spread those against a season. A northern lawn care company earning across seven months pays for twelve. The LawnPro Plus figure of $2,988 is really $2,988 recovered from seven months of invoices, which is $427 a month of season rather than $249.

That reframing matters most for the banded pricing model. Jobber lets you remove seats when seasonal staff leave, so a company that drops from ten to four in November stops paying for six seats. LawnPro bands by employee count, so moving from Plus back to Startup for the winter is a plan change rather than an adjustment, and most operators do not bother.

For a genuinely seasonal operation, per seat pricing is the better structure despite the higher headline price. This is the one place on this site where the per seat model wins the argument.

## What to check before you commit

Three questions decide this category and none of them are answered on a pricing page.

Ask how a rained off day is handled. The operation you will perform most is pushing an entire day forward, not rescheduling one visit, and tools differ enormously in how painful that is.

Ask what happens to your route when a crew is one person short. A schedule that has to be rebuilt by hand at six in the morning is a schedule you will abandon by July.

Ask how annual agreements renew. Lawn care sells a season, and a system that treats each visit as an independent job will make you resell the same customer every spring.

## Where each tool is the wrong choice

**LawnPro is wrong** if you need automated reminders on a budget, because they require the $129 Grow plan, and wrong if you plan to cross seven employees soon, because the next tier is $249.

**Jobber is wrong** for pure mow and go route work at ten staff. At $400 a month on Connect you are paying for quoting depth that a fixed price recurring round does not use. Note that Grow includes five users at $199, so ten users cost $344 on Grow against $400 on Connect.

**Arborgold is wrong** for straightforward lawn maintenance. It is built for tree care and landscape construction where materials and job costing matter, and its licence pricing is not published.

**Yardbook is wrong** as a purchase you can research properly. It is widely recommended as the free option in lawn care and its pricing page blocked automated access in August 2026, so nothing about its cost is verified here.

**Service Autopilot is wrong** for anyone who needs customer facing booking at a known price. The portal is a call for pricing item on top of a plan that already costs $199 at the Pro tier.

**Aspire is wrong** for every business this site is written for. It gives no price, stating only that cost varies by company size and complexity, and its plans page is built around a conversation with sales rather than a number. User licences are unlimited, which tells you it is sold to companies large enough for seat counts to be the wrong unit.

## Common questions

**What is the best lawn care software for a small business?**
LawnPro at $39 a month for up to three employees, which is the cheapest published price in this category that includes a client portal and unlimited customers. Jobber at $139 is stronger if you quote a lot of new work.

**Is there genuinely free lawn care software?**
LawnPro's Solo plan is free for one user and twenty five customers. Yardbook is widely described as free, and its pricing page could not be accessed in August 2026, so that is not verified here.

**Why can I not find a price for Service Autopilot or Aspire?**
Because both route it through a sales conversation. Service Autopilot publishes plan prices but lists the client portal as call for pricing. Aspire states that pricing varies by company size and complexity, and gives no figure, though it does include unlimited user licences.

**What does a lawn care software app need that general software does not?**
Route density and seasonal contract renewal. A lawn care round is a sequence of short visits where drive time dominates, and the contract repeats annually rather than being quoted each time.

**How current are these prices?**
They were read from vendor pricing pages in August 2026. Pricing in this category changes several times a year, so reconfirm before committing.
