For a cleaning business under twenty staff, Wave at $19 a month is the cheapest option that will both invoice clients and keep a ledger, and FreshBooks at $43 a month is the cheapest that will invoice fifty recurring clients and chase late payment automatically. Neither will schedule a clean. Figures were read from the vendor pricing pages in August 2026 and are the lowest tier that includes recurring invoicing and card payments.

The important distinction on this page is not price. It is that most tools sold as accounting software for a cleaning business are invoicing tools, and most tools sold as cleaning software contain an invoice generator that is not accounting. Buying either one believing it is the other is how cleaning companies arrive at tax season with a shoebox.

## An invoice generator is not a ledger

An invoicing tool produces a document, records that it was paid, and stops. A ledger records the payment against a chart of accounts, tracks the expense side, reconciles against the bank feed and produces a profit and loss statement your accountant can work from.

ZenMaid and Jobber generate invoices. They are not ledgers and do not claim to be. Jobber's own position is that it syncs to an accounting package rather than being one.

Wave, FreshBooks and QuickBooks Online are on the other side of the line. None of them will schedule a clean, route a van or tell a cleaner which house is next.

A cleaning company with staff will end up running one from each column. The realistic question is which pair costs least in total.

## What the pairs actually cost

At three cleaners, ZenMaid at $39 plus Wave at $19 comes to $58 a month, and covers booking, scheduling, reminders, invoicing and a real ledger.

The same three cleaners on Jobber Connect at $197 a month still need an accounting package underneath it, because Jobber syncs rather than reconciles. The comparison is $58 against $197 plus a ledger.

At ten cleaners the gap widens, because ZenMaid and Wave are flat and Jobber is $29 for every seat. Ten users on Jobber Connect is $400 a month.

FreshBooks sits between the two. At $43 a month on the Plus plan you get fifty billable clients and automated late payment reminders, which matters for recurring residential work where the same forty households are billed every month. Each additional team member is $11, so ten users reach $142.

## Reading the client caps before the price

FreshBooks advertises Lite at $23 a month. Lite is capped at five billable clients. A cleaning company with five clients is a person with a bucket, and the moment you have a sixth recurring customer that plan is finished. Plus at $43 covers fifty. Unlimited requires Premium at $70.

This is the same pattern as the booking tier elsewhere on this site. The advertised number describes a business smaller than the one reading about it.

Wave's Starter plan is genuinely free and carries no invoice limit. The Pro plan at $19 a month is what adds automated late payment reminders, which for a cleaning round is the feature that recovers its own cost within one chased invoice. Wave's pricing page states no user limit at all, which is why the same $19 appears at one, three and ten cleaners. It does not confirm whether recurring invoice schedules are included, so treat that one point as unverified.

## Payroll is the line nobody prices

Cleaning is labour. For most cleaning companies payroll is between half and two thirds of revenue, which makes it the largest number in the accounts and the one most likely to be handled badly.

None of the accounting tools compared here includes payroll in the prices shown. Payroll is generally a separate product or a paid add on, and it is the most common reason a cleaning company's software bill turns out higher than planned.

This matters more in cleaning than in most trades because of how the work is structured. Cleaners are often part time, hours vary week to week, and travel between houses may or may not be paid depending on how you have set things up. Getting hours from a phone into a payroll run without retyping them is worth real money once you have more than about four cleaners.

Connecteam, which appears elsewhere on this site as a free workforce tool for up to ten users, tracks hours and produces payroll reporting. It does not run payroll. It gives you a defensible set of hours to run payroll from, which is the harder half of the problem for a cleaning company.

The practical structure for a company of five to fifteen cleaners is therefore three parts rather than two. A field tool that books and schedules, an hours record the cleaners actually use, and an accounting package that holds the ledger and connects to whatever runs payroll.

## The twelve month number

Annualised, the accounting side is the cheapest part of a cleaning company's software.

Wave Pro is $228 a year at any crew size shown. FreshBooks Plus is $516 a year for one user, $780 for three and $1,704 for ten. ZenMaid is $468 a year flat and generates invoices without keeping books. Jobber Connect is $1,668 a year for one user and $4,800 for ten, and still needs an accounting package underneath it.

The cheapest complete pairing for a three cleaner company is ZenMaid at $468 plus Wave at $228, which is $696 a year for booking, scheduling, reminders, invoicing and a real ledger.

The same three cleaners on Jobber Connect at $2,364 a year plus Wave at $228 is $2,592. The difference is $1,896 a year, and Jobber's quoting and reporting have to be worth that much to justify the choice.

## Where each tool is the wrong choice

**Wave is wrong** if you have a bookkeeper who expects a specific chart of accounts or if you need job costing per clean. It is a small ledger with no seat limit, which is a real advantage, and it will still not tell you which cleans made money.

**ZenMaid is wrong** as your only financial record. It bills cleans. It does not track expenses, mileage, supplies or payroll, and no accountant will accept it as a set of books.

**FreshBooks is wrong** below fifty clients if you buy Lite by mistake, and wrong above fifty unless you move to Premium at $70. It is also wrong if you want the accounting and the scheduling in one place, because it will never schedule.

**Jobber is wrong** as accounting software, full stop. It is a field service system with invoicing attached. At $400 a month for ten users it is also the most expensive way on this page to produce an invoice.

**QuickBooks Online is wrong** for anyone who cannot confirm the current price before committing. ## Common questions

**What is the cheapest accounting software for a cleaning business?**
Wave, which has a free Starter plan and charges $19 a month for the Pro plan that adds automated late payment reminders, as checked in August 2026.

**Do I need accounting software if my cleaning app already sends invoices?**
Yes, once you have staff. An invoice record is not a ledger. It does not hold expenses, payroll or reconciliation, which are the three things an accountant needs and the three things a cleaning app does not store.

**Is QuickBooks necessary for a small cleaning company?**
Not necessarily. It is necessary if your accountant requires it, which is common enough that it is worth asking before you choose anything else.

**What does card processing cost on top of the subscription?**
Wave publishes 2.9% plus $0.60 per credit card transaction, and 2.9% with no fixed fee on the first ten transactions each month on the Pro plan. Processing fees are separate from subscription cost at every vendor on this page.

**Does cleaning management software include accounting?**
No, and the marketing rarely says so plainly. Cleaning management software generates invoices and records that they were paid. It holds no chart of accounts, no expense side and no bank reconciliation, which are the three things your accountant needs. Budget for both.

**How should I split the work between two tools?**
Let the cleaning tool own the schedule, the booking and the invoice that goes to the customer. Let the accounting tool own the bank feed, the expenses and the reporting. Sync them once and do not try to keep two sets of customer records by hand.
