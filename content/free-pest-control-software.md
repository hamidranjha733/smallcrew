There is no free pest control software that records chemical applications, tracks devices and schedules a route. What exists is genuinely free software for parts of the job. Connecteam is free for up to ten users and will roster your technicians. Wave is free and will invoice your customers and keep a ledger. Neither holds a pesticide application record. The cheapest complete pest control system that publishes a price is GorillaDesk at $99 a month, checked in August 2026.

Pest control management software with a free plan does not exist for a reason worth understanding, because it explains what you are actually buying when you eventually pay.

## Why nothing in this category is free

Free software tiers exist where the marginal cost of a user is close to zero and where the vendor expects to sell you something later. That model works for invoicing and for staff messaging.

Pest control software carries obligations that make it expensive to support. The application record has to be accurate and retrievable for years. Device and station histories accumulate. State requirements differ. A vendor offering that free would be underwriting a compliance liability for customers paying nothing.

So the free options in this trade are always tools that solve a general problem, and the pest control specific parts are always paid.

## What the two free tools genuinely do

**Connecteam** is free for up to ten users on its Small Business plan, described as covering all hubs and all features, and this is a permanent plan rather than a trial. For a pest control operator with ten or fewer people it will schedule shifts, track hours, hold site instructions, run checklists and let technicians report issues with photographs.

That is a real portion of the operational problem, at no cost. What it will not do is take a customer booking, produce an invoice, hold a customer record or store a chemical application. It is the crew half of a system.

**Wave** has a free Starter plan with unlimited invoices and bookkeeping records. Card processing is 2.9% plus $0.60 per transaction. The Pro plan at $19 a month adds automated late payment reminders that deploy three, seven and fourteen days after an invoice becomes overdue.

That covers billing and the ledger. It has no route, no schedule and no connection to the work performed, so every invoice is raised by hand.

Running Connecteam and Wave together costs nothing and covers rostering, invoicing and accounts. It leaves the two things that define pest control uncovered, which are the route and the application record.

## What free actually costs

At a small enough size, free is correct. An operator working alone with twenty customers should not pay $99 a month for GorillaDesk.

The cost appears at the point where you are typing the same customer into two systems, raising invoices by hand from a paper route sheet, and keeping application records in a notebook. Each of those is time, and the notebook is also a compliance exposure.

The crossover is usually somewhere between thirty and sixty recurring customers. Below that, free tools plus discipline are defensible. Above it, the manual reconciliation costs more per month than the software does, and the application record has become a genuine risk rather than an inconvenience.

## The crossover point, calculated

The point at which free stops being cheap can be estimated rather than guessed.

Running Connecteam and Wave together, the manual work is the joining. Customers exist in Wave for billing and in your head or a spreadsheet for scheduling. Every completed stop has to become an invoice by hand. Every application has to be written down and filed.

Estimate three minutes per recurring customer per month for that reconciliation, which is conservative once queries and corrections are included.

At thirty customers that is ninety minutes a month. At a hundred it is five hours. At two hundred it is ten hours a month, or a full working day and a half.

GorillaDesk Pro at $99 a month removes most of it. Against ten hours a month of your own time, $99 is a straightforward purchase. Against ninety minutes, it is not obviously worth it.

So the crossover sits between roughly forty and eighty recurring customers depending on what you value your own time at. Below forty, free tools plus discipline are genuinely the right answer and anyone telling you otherwise is selling something. Above eighty, you are paying more in unbilled hours than the software costs.

## The compliance exposure that free does not cover

There is a second consideration that arithmetic does not capture.

Application records kept in a notebook are legal in most circumstances and fragile in all of them. They are difficult to search, easy to lose, and unhelpful under scrutiny. The moment a regulator, an insurer or a commercial client asks for two years of application history for a specific address, the notebook becomes a serious problem.

This is not a reason to buy software on day one. A single operator with twenty residential customers and a tidy notebook is fine.

It is a reason to treat the move to paid software as a compliance decision rather than a convenience one, and to make it before you take your first commercial contract rather than after. Commercial clients in food handling, healthcare and property management routinely audit pest control records, and a paper file will not survive that.

The honest summary is that free tools run a small pest control business well and do not run a growing one, and the constraint that ends the arrangement is usually the record rather than the workload.

## Where each tool is the wrong choice

**Connecteam is wrong** as a pest control system. It is a workforce tool. It cannot book, invoice, or record a treatment, and no configuration will change that.

**Wave is wrong** for any route large enough that manual invoicing hurts, which is most operators past about thirty customers.

**GorillaDesk is wrong** for an operator who genuinely cannot afford $99 a month, and there is no free tier to fall back to. At ten separate technician schedules it reaches $549.

**Jobber is wrong** for pest control regardless of price, because it holds no chemical application log. Its free trial is a trial, not a free plan.

**Briostack is wrong** on this page entirely. Its published material covers add on and migration charges but no base subscription price and no free tier, so it cannot be assessed against a budget of nothing.

## Common questions

**Is there any genuinely free pest control software?**
Not for the pest control specific parts. Connecteam is free for up to ten users for staff scheduling, and Wave is free for invoicing and bookkeeping. Neither records chemical applications or manages a service route.

**What is the cheapest paid pest control software?**
GorillaDesk publishes Basic at $49 a month, though the customer portal that allows online booking begins on Pro at $99. Those were the published figures in August 2026.

**Can I run a pest control business on spreadsheets and free tools?**
Up to roughly thirty recurring customers, yes, provided your application records are kept properly. Past that the duplicate data entry costs more than the software, and paper application records become a real liability.

**Why does Connecteam give ten users the full platform free?**
It is a permanent Small Business plan covering all hubs and features for up to ten users. It is genuinely free, and it is a workforce management product rather than a field service one.

**How current are these prices?**
They were read from vendor pricing pages in August 2026. Vendor pricing changes several times a year.
